# Neural constructive and improvement methods for combinatorial optimization (TSP, VRP, scheduling, MIS / Max-Cut / MVC), 2015 to late 2026

Scope note: end-to-end neural solvers that build a solution (learning to construct, L2C), predict a heatmap or solution distribution that a decoder or search turns into a solution (learning to predict, L2P), or refine a solution (learning to search or improve, L2S). This note does not cover learned branching in MIP solvers or LLM code generation, apart from a short pointer. Research was done 2026-11-08. Where an arXiv ID or venue is given without "(verified)", it comes from the paper's arXiv listing. Numbers marked "POMO Table 2/3" were read directly from the POMO paper (Kwon et al., NeurIPS 2020, arXiv 2010.16011, pages 7-8).

Surveys for background: Bengio, Lodi, Prouvost, "Machine learning for combinatorial optimization: a methodological tour d'horizon" (EJOR 2021) — [arXiv 1811.06128](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.06128); Cappart et al., "Combinatorial optimization and reasoning with graph neural networks" (JMLR 2023) — [arXiv 2102.09544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09544); Mazyavkina et al., "Reinforcement learning for combinatorial optimization: a survey" (Computers & OR 2021) — [arXiv 2003.03600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03600); a survey of NCO for VRPs, online in IEEE TNNLS July 2026 — [arXiv 2406.00415](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.00415), [PubMed 42479518](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42479518/).

## 1. Foundational works (2015-2022) and what each contributed

### Takeaway
The autoregressive L2C line runs from Pointer Networks (2015, supervised) to Bello et al. (2016, RL plus active search), S2V-DQN (2017, graph embedding plus Q-learning), the Attention Model (Kool et al., ICLR 2019, a transformer trained with REINFORCE and a greedy-rollout baseline) and POMO (NeurIPS 2020, multiple-start rollouts as a shared baseline plus 8-fold augmentation). POMO cut the TSP100 gap from about 2-3.5% (AM) to 0.14%, and on CVRP100 to 0.32% versus LKH3. Later work (Sym-NCO, MatNet, EAS, SGBS) adds symmetry, other problem families and test-time search on top of the AM/POMO backbone.

### Cited Findings
- **Pointer Networks** (Vinyals, Fortunato, Jaitly; NeurIPS 2015): an attention mechanism used as a pointer over input positions, so the output vocabulary grows with input size. Trained by supervision on convex hull, Delaunay triangulation and small TSP (up to n=50) — [arXiv 1506.03134](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03134)
- **Bello, Pham, Le, Norouzi, Bengio, "Neural Combinatorial Optimization with Reinforcement Learning"** (arXiv Nov 2016; ICLR 2017 workshop track). Trains the pointer network with policy-gradient RL (actor-critic) on tour length, so no optimal labels are needed. Introduces "active search", which fine-tunes on the test instance. Covers TSP up to 100 and knapsack — [arXiv 1611.09940](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09940)
- **Nazari et al.** (NeurIPS 2018): replace the RNN encoder with a permutation-invariant embedding to handle the dynamic demands of VRP — [arXiv 1802.04240](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04240)
- **Khalil, Dai, Zhang, Dilkina, Song, "Learning Combinatorial Optimization Algorithms over Graphs" (S2V-DQN)** (NeurIPS 2017): a structure2vec graph embedding and n-step fitted Q-learning drive a greedy node-insertion policy for MVC, Max-Cut and TSP. This is the first general graph-RL greedy meta-algorithm — [arXiv 1704.01665](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.01665)
- **Li, Chen, Koltun** (NeurIPS 2018): a GCN predicts node likelihoods for MIS, used inside a guided tree search with local search and kernelization — [arXiv 1810.10659](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.10659). Re-examined by Böther et al. (ICLR 2022): the GCN "can in fact be replaced by random values". The search's performance comes from kernelization and other algorithmic techniques, so the original results are not reproducible, and classical solvers are often faster at similar quality — [arXiv 2201.10494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10494) (verified)
- **Kool, van Hoof, Welling, "Attention, Learn to Solve Routing Problems!" (AM)** (ICLR 2019): a transformer encoder over nodes and an autoregressive pointer-style decoder with context. Trained with REINFORCE using a greedy-rollout baseline from a frozen copy of the policy. Applied to TSP, CVRP, split-delivery VRP, orienteering and prize-collecting TSP — [arXiv 1803.08475](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08475)
- AM performance as re-measured in POMO Table 2 (AM retrained with REINFORCE and the greedy-rollout baseline, 10,000 test instances): TSP100 greedy 3.51% gap (2 s total), sampling 1.98% (22 min); TSP50 greedy 1.21%; TSP20 0.19%. POMO Table 3 (against LKH3): CVRP100 greedy 6.72% (3 s), sampling 3.09% (30 min) — [POMO, arXiv 2010.16011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.16011) (verified from the PDF tables)
- **POMO** (Kwon et al., NeurIPS 2020): each node is a starting point for a separate rollout (N trajectories). The mean reward of the N rollouts is the shared REINFORCE baseline, and inference applies 8-fold coordinate augmentation (flips and rotations). TSP results (Concorde reference): TSP20 0.00%, TSP50 0.03% (16 s), TSP100 0.14% (1 min, x8 augmentation). Without augmentation: TSP100 0.46% (11 s); single trajectory: 1.07% (2 s) — [arXiv 2010.16011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.16011) (verified)
- POMO CVRP (LKH3 reference): CVRP20 0.21%, CVRP50 0.45%, CVRP100 0.32% (2 min for 10,000 instances, x8 augmentation). LKH3 needed 12 h for CVRP100, OR-Tools 9.34% in 1 h — [arXiv 2010.16011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.16011) (verified)
- Baselines in the same POMO tables, useful as the 2019-2020 reference point. On TSP100: Concorde 1 h, LKH3 25 min, Gurobi 17 min, OR-Tools 3.86%, farthest insertion 7.59%, GCN beam search (Joshi et al.) 1.39% (40 min), Wu et al. improvement with 5000 steps 1.42% (2 h), da Costa et al. 2-opt with 2000 steps 0.87% (41 min). On CVRP100 (objective; LKH3 = 15.68): NeuRewriter 16.10 (1 h), NLNS 15.99 (1 h), L2I 15.57 (4000 h), i.e. better than LKH3 but at an enormous compute cost — [arXiv 2010.16011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.16011) (verified)
- **Sym-NCO** (Kim, Park, Park; NeurIPS 2022): a regularizer that exploits problem symmetry (multiple equivalent solution representations, rotation and reflection invariance of instances) on top of REINFORCE. It is architecture-agnostic and is applied to POMO, PointerNet, AM and others — [arXiv 2205.13209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13209)
- **MatNet** (Kwon et al., NeurIPS 2021): encodes bipartite matrix inputs (distance or processing-time matrices), extending POMO-style solvers to asymmetric TSP and the flexible flow-shop scheduling problem — [arXiv 2106.11113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11113)
- **L2D** (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2020): a GNN on the disjunctive graph and a PPO policy that learns priority dispatching rules for job-shop scheduling — [arXiv 2010.12367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12367)
- **Efficient Active Search (EAS)** (Hottung, Kwon, Tierney; ICLR 2022): at test time, updates only a subset of parameters (embeddings, an added layer, or a lookup table) per instance. This makes Bello's active search practical on top of POMO — [arXiv 2106.05126](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05126)
- **SGBS** (Choo et al., NeurIPS 2022): simulation-guided beam search, which expands a beam and scores candidates by greedy rollouts of the policy. Combined with EAS (SGBS+EAS) — [arXiv 2207.06190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06190)

### Inferences
- Most of POMO's gain over AM comes from training: the same network goes from 3.51% to 1.07% on TSP100 with a single trajectory. Inference tricks (multi-start, augmentation) give another order of magnitude, from 1.07% to 0.14%. Later constructive solvers mostly keep the POMO training scheme.
- In 2020, neural constructive solvers beat OR-Tools but not LKH3. Their selling point was solving 10,000 small instances in about a minute on a GPU. That batch-throughput framing still underlies most claimed speed advantages.

### Gaps
- Kool et al.'s own TSP100 figures (often quoted as 4.53% greedy and 2.26% sampling with 1280 samples) were not re-read in this session. The POMO table reports AM as retrained by the POMO authors.
- Exact Sym-NCO, EAS and SGBS gap numbers were not re-verified; see each paper's tables.
- PolyNet (Hottung et al., ICLR 2024), COMPASS (Chalumeau et al., NeurIPS 2023) and MEMENTO, all diversity- or memory-based POMO successors, were not verified in this session.

## 2. Non-autoregressive / heatmap, diffusion, unsupervised and GFlowNet approaches

### Takeaway
Heatmap methods predict edge or node probabilities in one shot: supervised GCN (Joshi 2019), then the meta-learned DIMES (2022), the diffusion model DIFUSCO (2023), T2T with gradient search (2023) and the consistency model Fast T2T (2024). They then decode with greedy, sampling, 2-opt or MCTS. They reported TSP-500/1000/10000 gaps of about 0.5%/1.2%/2.6%. The ICML 2024 position paper by Xia et al. showed that a parameter-free SoftDist heatmap (a softmax over distances) matches or beats most learned heatmaps under the same MCTS, which questions how much the learning contributes. Unsupervised methods (Erdős Goes Neural, PI-GNN, DiffUCO, SDDS, RLD) and GFlowNets cover MIS, Max-Cut, MVC and clique. Strong critiques show that simple greedy or classical solvers often match or beat them.

### Cited Findings
- **Joshi, Laurent, Bresson** (2019): a residual gated GCN, trained by supervision on Concorde tours, outputs an edge-probability heatmap for TSP that is decoded by greedy, beam search or beam search with shortest-tour selection. TSP100 gap 1.39% with beam search (40 min for 10,000 instances, POMO Table 2) — [arXiv 1906.01227](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01227); [POMO table](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.16011)
- **Att-GCN+MCTS** (Fu, Qiu, Zha; AAAI 2021): heatmaps predicted on small subgraphs and merged, then used to guide MCTS (k-opt search) on large TSP. This is the template that DIMES, DIFUSCO and UTSP reuse at scale — [arXiv 2012.10658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10658)
- **DIMES** (Qiu, Sun, Yang; NeurIPS 2022): a continuous parameterization of the solution distribution, trained by RL with meta-learning for fast per-instance fine-tuning. Scales heatmap-plus-MCTS to TSP-10000 — [arXiv 2210.04123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04123)
- **DIFUSCO** (Sun, Yang; NeurIPS 2023 spotlight): a graph-based discrete or continuous diffusion model that generates edge or node indicators. The neural-versus-ground-truth gap moves from the prior state of the art to DIFUSCO as follows: TSP-500 1.76% to 0.46%, TSP-1000 2.46% to 1.17%, TSP-10000 3.19% to 2.58%. It also beats prior neural solvers on MIS (SATLIB) — [arXiv 2302.08224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08224) (verified)
- **T2T** (Li et al., NeurIPS 2023) adds objective-guided gradient search at test time to diffusion. **Fast T2T** (Li, Guo, Wang, Zha, Yan; NeurIPS 2024, arXiv Feb 2025) adds optimization consistency models: with one-step generation and one-step gradient search it "can mostly outperform" diffusion solvers that take hundreds of steps, with "tens of times speedup", and reportedly beats LKH under limited time budgets on TSP and MIS — [arXiv 2502.02941](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.02941); [NeurIPS 2024](https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2024/li2024neurips-fast); code [thinklab-sjtu/t2tco](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fthinklab-sjtu%2Ft2tco)
- **Xia et al., "Position: Rethinking Post-Hoc Search-Based Neural Approaches for Solving Large-Scale TSP"** (ICML 2024 oral). SoftDist, a softmax over the distance matrix with one temperature tuned by grid search, beats most complex ML heatmaps (Att-GCN, DIMES, DIFUSCO, UTSP) in quality and speed when fed to the same MCTS. The authors question the practical value of heatmap-guided MCTS — [arXiv 2406.03503](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03503v1); [ICML oral](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/oral/35505); [code](https://github.com/xyfffff/rethink_mcts_for_tsp)
- Rebuttal by the UTSP author: SoftDist's timing excludes heatmap I/O while UTSP's includes it, so the comparison is unfair — [arXiv 2406.09441](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09441). Xia et al.'s README responds that even an all-zero heatmap beats UTSP on TSP-500 once the MCTS parameters are tuned — [GitHub](https://github.com/xyfffff/rethink_mcts_for_tsp)
- **Erdős Goes Neural** (Karalias, Loukas; NeurIPS 2020): unsupervised training. The GNN outputs Bernoulli probabilities, and the loss is a probabilistic-method penalty bound that guarantees a feasible, good-quality integral solution via derandomization (sequential decoding). Applied to max-clique and constrained min-cut — [arXiv 2006.10643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10643)
- **PI-GNN** (Schuetz, Brubaker, Katzgraber; Nature Machine Intelligence 2022): per-instance unsupervised training of a GNN on a QUBO/Ising relaxation for MIS and Max-Cut — [arXiv 2107.01188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01188). Critique by Angelini and Ricci-Tersenghi (Nature MI 2023): on random regular graphs a simple greedy algorithm finds much larger independent sets and runs about 10^4 times faster on million-variable graphs — [arXiv 2206.13211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13211v2). Reply: random regular graphs are not a universal benchmark, and on hard dense instances these GNNs can beat greedy — [arXiv 2302.03602](https://export.arxiv.org/abs/2302.03602)
- **DiffUCO** (Sanokowski, Hochreiter, Lehner; ICML 2024): data-free diffusion for unsupervised CO, trained on a joint variational upper bound of the reverse KL. Benchmarks are MIS, max clique, MDS, Max-Cut and MVC — [arXiv 2406.01661](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2406.01661). **SDDS** (Sanokowski et al., 2025) scales discrete diffusion samplers with reverse-KL plus RL and forward-KL plus MC objectives. More diffusion steps at inference improve quality — [arXiv 2502.08696](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08696)
- **Regularized Langevin Dynamics** (Feng, Yang; 2025): enforces an expected distance between sampled and current solutions to escape local minima, as RLSA (in simulated annealing) and RLNN (an unsupervised neural network) — [arXiv 2502.00277](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00277v4). **CAM** (combinatorial adjoint matching, May 2026 preprint): an unsupervised diffusion solver on MIS, TSP, Max-Cut and CVRP that argues SDDS's local objective misses long-range dependencies — [arXiv 2605.30920](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30920)
- **GFlowNets for CO** (Zhang et al., "Let the Flows Tell", NeurIPS 2023): GFlowNets over Markov decision processes that construct solutions, for MIS, Max-Clique, MDS and Max-Cut — [arXiv 2305.17010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17010)
- **DeepACO** (Ye et al., NeurIPS 2023): a neural network learns ant colony optimization heuristic matrices (a heatmap) for TSP, CVRP and others — [arXiv 2309.14032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14032)

### Inferences
- Heatmap and diffusion results at TSP-500 and above are hard to attribute to learning, because the decoder (2-opt or MCTS) does most of the work. The SoftDist and zero-heatmap results make "same decoder, non-learned prior" a necessary ablation. Later papers (Fast T2T) moved toward gradient search and consistency models partly in response.
- On MIS and Max-Cut, both critiques (Böther 2022; Angelini and Ricci-Tersenghi 2023) say greedy or KaMIS-type classical baselines must be run at matched time. Unsupervised GNN results without them are not reliable evidence.

### Gaps
- Concrete DIMES, T2T, UTSP and GFlowNet gap numbers were not re-read.
- Whether DiffUCO and SDDS beat KaMIS or Gurobi at matched time was not verified here.
- Venues for SDDS and RLD (possibly ICLR or ICML 2025) were not confirmed by search.

## 3. Improvement / local-search learning (L2S)

### Takeaway
L2S methods learn which move to apply in a local search: NeuRewriter (region plus rule picking, 2019), Wu et al. (2-opt, swap and relocate pair selection, 2019/2021), Learning 2-opt (da Costa 2020), L2I (2020), NLNS (learned destroy-and-repair, 2020), and NeuOpt (flexible k-opt with infeasible-region exploration, 2023). Hybrids that keep a classical solver and learn only a component (NeuroLKH: GNN-predicted candidate edges plus penalties inside LKH, 2021) have proved the most robust. Pure L2S matched or beat LKH3 on CVRP100 only at very large compute (L2I: 4000 h for 10,000 instances).

### Cited Findings
- **NeuRewriter** (Chen, Tian; NeurIPS 2019): learns a region-picking and a rule-picking policy that iteratively rewrite a solution. Applied to expression simplification, online job scheduling and CVRP. CVRP100 objective 16.10 versus LKH3 15.68 (POMO Table 3) — [arXiv 1810.00337](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00337); [POMO table](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.16011)
- **Wu, Song, Cao, Zhang, Lim, "Learning Improvement Heuristics for Solving Routing Problems"** (arXiv 2019; IEEE TNNLS 2021): a self-attention policy picks node pairs for 2-opt, swap or relocate operators, trained with RL. TSP100 1.42% with 5000 steps (2 h) — [arXiv 1912.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05784); [POMO table](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.16011)
- **Learning 2-opt** (da Costa, Rhuggenaath, Zhang, Akcay; ACML 2020): a pointer-attention policy picks 2-opt moves. TSP100 0.87% with 2000 steps (41 min, POMO Table 2) — [arXiv 2004.01608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01608)
- **L2I** (Lu, Zhang, Yang; ICLR 2020): an RL controller over a pool of improvement and perturbation operators for CVRP. CVRP100 15.57, below LKH3's 15.68, but at about 4000 h for the test set (POMO Table 3) — [POMO table](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.16011)
- **NLNS** (Hottung, Tierney; ECAI 2020): neural large neighborhood search, with handcrafted destroy operators and a learned attention-based repair. CVRP100 15.99 (1 h, POMO Table 3) — [arXiv 1911.09539](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.09539)
- **NeuOpt** (Ma, Cao, Chee; NeurIPS 2023): learns flexible k-opt (any k >= 2) through action factorization and a recurrent dual-stream decoder. **GIRE** (Guided Infeasible Region Exploration) adds feasibility features and reward shaping instead of hard masking, and **D2A** (dynamic data augmentation) is used at inference. It reportedly outstrips masking-based L2S solvers and beats L2C and L2P solvers on TSP and CVRP — [arXiv 2310.18264](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.18264); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/9bae70d354793a95fa18751888cea07d-Abstract.html)
- **NeuroLKH** (Xin, Song, Cao, Zhang; NeurIPS 2021): a sparse GNN trained to predict edge scores (supervised) and node penalties (unsupervised) that replace LKH's alpha-nearness candidate sets and subgradient penalties. It improves on LKH at equal time and generalizes to larger TSPs and TSPLIB — [arXiv 2110.07983](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.07983)
- **Destroy and Repair Using Hyper Graphs (DRHG)** (2025): a recent learned LNS for routing, with HGS as the CVRP reference in its tables — [arXiv 2502.16170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.16170)
- Test-time search on constructive policies (EAS, SGBS) is the other main improvement route. It refines the policy per instance rather than editing the solution — [EAS arXiv 2106.05126](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05126); [SGBS arXiv 2207.06190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06190)

### Inferences
- The improvement methods that hold up most broadly do not replace the classical solver. They make one component of a strong solver learned (candidate sets in LKH, operator choice, repair), so the solver's guarantees and its end-game quality are kept.
- Pure L2S solvers report results in "steps" (T = 1k-5k). Comparisons with LKH or HGS at equal wall-clock time are often missing or unfavorable at n >= 100.

### Gaps
- The DACT and N2S (Ma et al., NeurIPS 2021, TNNLS 2022) numbers and the NeuOpt-versus-HGS figures were not extracted.
- NeuroLKH's gap reductions at fixed time were not re-read.

## 4. Large-scale generalization and multi-task / foundation models (2023-2026)

### Takeaway
Since 2023 the field has moved from "train on n=100, test on n=100" to size and distribution generalization and to large instances. Heavy-decoder or bisimulation models (LEHD, BQ-NCO, NeurIPS 2023) generalize from n=100 to n=1000. Divide-and-conquer hierarchies (H-TSP AAAI 2023, GLOP AAAI 2024, UDC 2024) and self-improved training with linear attention (Luo et al., ICLR 2025) reach 10K-100K nodes. GLOP reported about 5.1% on TSP100K at roughly 174x LKH-3's speed. Multi-task routing models (MTPOMO KDD 2024, MVMoE ICML 2024, RouteFinder TMLR 2025 with 48 variants, CaDA, GOAL) are the "foundation model" thread. 2025-2026 work targets real-world asymmetric costs (RRNCO), vision-assisted multi-task models and benchmark realism (FrontierCO, ICLR 2026).

### Cited Findings
- **LEHD** (Luo, Lin, Liu, Zhang, Wang; NeurIPS 2023): Light Encoder and Heavy Decoder, which re-encodes the remaining nodes at every step and is trained by supervision. Trained on small instances, it gives near-optimal TSP and CVRP solutions up to n=1000 and generalizes to TSPLib and CVRPLib — [arXiv 2310.07985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07985) (verified)
- **BQ-NCO** (Drakulic et al., NeurIPS 2023): bisimulation-quotiented MDPs reduce each partial-solution state to the remaining subproblem, giving a heavy-decoder model that generalizes across sizes for TSP, CVRP, OP, KP and others — [arXiv 2301.03313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03313)
- **H-TSP** (Pan et al., AAAI 2023): hierarchical RL. An upper level picks subproblems and a lower level solves them with an AM-like policy, for TSP up to 10,000 nodes — [arXiv 2304.09395](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.09395)
- **GLOP** (Ye et al., AAAI 2024): non-autoregressive global partition plus autoregressive local construction. TSP100K gap about 5.1%, roughly 174x faster than single-run LKH-3. The authors state that GLOP does not compete with iterative-improvement methods such as LKH-3 on solution quality; its niche is real-time solving — [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30009/31772); [arXiv 2312.08224](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.08224v1) (verified)
- **UDC** (Zheng, Zhou, Tong, Yuan, Wang): a unified neural divide-and-conquer framework. A GNN divides the instance and a fixed-length sub-path solver conquers the parts, trained with Divide-Conquer-Reunion (DCR) to correct a sub-optimal dividing policy. Evaluated on 10 large-scale CO problems. The arXiv page (v4, Jan 2025) lists no venue — [arXiv 2407.00312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00312) (verified)
- **Luo et al., "Boosting Neural Combinatorial Optimization for Large-Scale Vehicle Routing Problems"** (ICLR 2025; preprint "Self-Improved Learning for Scalable NCO", arXiv March 2024). Self-improved training uses the model's own locally reconstructed solutions as pseudo-labels, so no optimal labels are needed. Attention is linear in n via representative nodes. Tested on TSP and CVRP up to 100K nodes, including real-world distributions. The authors note that BQ and LEHD cannot handle 10K-100K instances — [ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/264f2e10479c9370972847e96107db7f-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2403.19561](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.19561v2)
- **LocalEscaper** (2025): a weakly supervised framework with regional reconstruction, evaluated on TSP-20000 and TSP-50000 against LKH-3 — [arXiv 2502.12484](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12484). **Learning to reduce search space for generalizable neural routing** (2025) — [arXiv 2503.03137](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.03137). **Test-time projection learning** for VRP generalization (2025) — [arXiv 2506.02392](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02392)
- Generalization lines include Omni-generalization via meta-learning (Zhou et al., ICML 2023, [arXiv 2305.19587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19587)), INViT (invariant nested-view transformer, ICML 2024, [arXiv 2402.02317](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02317)), ELG (ensemble of local and global policies, IJCAI 2024, [arXiv 2308.14104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14104)) and ICAM (instance-conditioned adaptation, 2024, [arXiv 2405.01906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01906))
- **MTPOMO** (Liu et al., KDD 2024): multi-task VRP solver over attribute compositions (capacity, time windows, open routes, backhauls, duration limits), with zero-shot transfer to unseen combinations — [arXiv 2402.16891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16891)
- **MVMoE** (Zhou et al., ICML 2024, PMLR 235:61804-61824): mixture-of-experts layers with hierarchical gating. Better zero-shot generalization on 10 unseen VRP variants and decent few-shot and real-world benchmark results; hierarchical gating helps out of distribution — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/zhou24c.html); [arXiv 2405.01029](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.01029v2) (verified)
- **RouteFinder** (Berto et al.; TMLR 2025, earlier an oral at the ICML 2024 FM-Wild workshop; arXiv v1 June 2024, v4 Sept 2025): a unified environment over any combination of attributes covering 48 VRP variants. Components: a transformer encoder with global attribute embeddings, mixed batch training, multi-variant reward normalization, and efficient adapter layers for unseen attributes. It reportedly outperforms recent learning methods (MTPOMO, MVMoE) — [arXiv 2406.15007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15007) (verified)
- **CaDA** (Li et al., 2024/2025): a cross-problem solver with constraint prompts and constraint-aware dual attention, reaching state-of-the-art across 16 VRP variants (venue not confirmed) — [arXiv 2412.00346](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00346v1). A federated pre-train/fine-tune framework applied to MVMoE and CaDA over 10 VRPs appeared in April 2026 — [arXiv 2604.10652](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10652v1)
- **RRNCO** ("Towards Real-World Routing with NCO", ICLR 2026 submission): node-only architectures cannot represent asymmetric, correlated distance and duration matrices, so the paper adds adaptive node embeddings and bias terms for real-road instances — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=sKvo9ZZfpe)
- A "Vision-Assisted Foundation Model for Solving Multi-Task VRPs" (arXiv June 2026, listed for IEEE TNNLS), plus 2026 cross-problem and Combination-of-Experts multi-task solvers (one listed for ICLR 2026), appear in community trackers — [arXiv 2606.10431](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10431); [awesome-fm4co](https://github.com/ai4co/awesome-fm4co); [COPZoo](https://github.com/dongjinkun/COPZoo)
- LLM-driven heuristic design is a parallel thread in which LLMs evolve heuristic code rather than a network outputting solutions, e.g. EoH (Evolution of Heuristics, ICML 2024) — [arXiv 2401.02051](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.02051) — and ReEvo (NeurIPS 2024) — [arXiv 2402.01145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01145). ViTSP (2025) uses a vision-language model to pick subregions for LKH-3 to re-optimize. It reports 0.19-0.24% average gaps on TSPLIB instances up to 88K nodes versus 0.31% for LKH-3 with more runs, and cuts LKH-3's gap by 12-100% on instances above 10K nodes at equal time; it is a hybrid, not a pure neural solver — [arXiv 2509.23465](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.23465); [summary](https://chatpaper.com/paper/192908)
- Tooling: RL4CO (Berto et al.), a PyTorch library and benchmark that implements AM, POMO, Sym-NCO, MatNet, DeepACO, NeuOpt, MTPOMO/MVMoE/RouteFinder and others — [arXiv 2306.17100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17100)

### Inferences
- The scaling recipe that has converged by 2025-2026 uses decomposition (partition, then solve fixed-size subproblems, then reunify), linear or local attention, and label-free self-improvement. Pure end-to-end attention over all n nodes stops being practical above about 10K nodes.
- The "foundation model" claims in routing are, so far, multi-attribute VRP families (up to 48 variants) with adapters for new constraints, mostly on synthetic uniform instances of n <= 100-1000. RRNCO and benchmark efforts such as FrontierCO are early responses to the sim-to-real gap.

### Gaps
- Concrete gaps were not extracted for LEHD (often cited as about 0.58% greedy on TSP100 and near 0% with RRC), BQ-NCO, UDC, INViT, ELG, ICAM, MTPOMO, MVMoE and RouteFinder (vs HGS and PyVRP). The verified abstracts give only qualitative claims.
- UDC's venue (believed NeurIPS 2024) was not confirmed by the arXiv page. GOAL (Drakulic et al., generalist CO agent learner, possibly ICLR 2025) was not verified.
- No reliable result was found for the accepted ICLR 2026 multi-task VRP papers (titles or numbers). The tracker entries are listings only.

## 5. Gaps versus Concorde / LKH-3 / HGS, runtime, and where neural methods win or lose

### Takeaway
Neural solvers win on batch throughput for many small, same-distribution instances (POMO: 10,000 TSP100 instances in about 1 minute at 0.14%) and in real-time large-scale settings where some quality loss is acceptable (GLOP on TSP100K: about 5% gap, roughly 174x faster than LKH-3). They lose to LKH-3, HGS and Concorde on solution quality given modest time, and the gap widens with size, structure and distribution shift. FrontierCO (ICLR 2026) compared 16 ML solvers with classical solvers on instances up to 10M-node TSP and 8M-node MIS and found a "persistent performance gap that widens" on structurally hard and large instances, with only isolated cases where ML wins.

### Cited Findings
- Small-instance reference (POMO Tables 2-3, 10,000 instances). POMO x8: TSP100 0.14% in 1 min versus Concorde 1 h and LKH3 25 min (both 0%); CVRP100 0.32% versus LKH3 in 2 min versus 12 h — [arXiv 2010.16011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.16011) (verified)
- The only 2020-era neural method below LKH3 on CVRP100, L2I (15.57 vs 15.68), needed about 4000 h — [POMO table](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.16011) (verified)
- Mid-scale heatmap and diffusion results: DIFUSCO TSP-500 0.46%, TSP-1000 1.17%, TSP-10000 2.58% (with post-hoc search) — [arXiv 2302.08224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08224). The ICML 2024 position paper shows that a non-learned SoftDist heatmap matches or beats these under the same MCTS — [arXiv 2406.03503](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03503v1)
- Large scale: GLOP on TSP100K, 5.1% gap at about 174x the speed of single-run LKH-3; the authors concede that LKH-3 is better on quality — [AAAI 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30009/31772)
- **FrontierCO** (Feng, Sun, Li, Talwalkar, Yang; ICLR 2026; arXiv May 2025, v3 March 2026): eight CO problems (routing, scheduling, facility location, graph problems) with "easy" (formerly hard, now solvable) and "hard" (open or computationally intensive) sets from DIMACS, TSPLib and competitions. 16 ML solvers (GNN, hybrid neural-symbolic, LLM agents) versus state-of-the-art classical solvers show a persistent gap that widens on structurally hard and large instances, up to 10M-node TSP and 8M-node MIS, with some cases where ML wins — [arXiv 2505.16952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16952) (verified). Search snippets of the paper indicate that LKH-3 stays near-optimal in seconds up to about 18.5K nodes, while DIFUSCO, LEHD and SIL fall behind — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.16952) (snippet only, not verified in full text)
- MIS and graph problems: GCN-guided tree search results are not reproducible, and the GNN can be replaced by random values (ICLR 2022) — [arXiv 2201.10494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10494). Greedy beats PI-GNN on MIS and runs about 10^4 times faster on 10^6-variable graphs — [arXiv 2206.13211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13211v2)
- Fast T2T reports beating LKH under limited time budgets on TSP and MIS, i.e. a matched-short-time claim, not a converged-quality one — [arXiv 2502.02941](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.02941)
- Hybrids are where neural guidance beats LKH-3 at equal time on very large TSPLIB instances. ViTSP reports 0.19-0.24% versus 0.31% for LKH-3 on instances up to 88K nodes, with LKH-3 still the inner solver — [arXiv 2509.23465](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.23465). NeuroLKH (learned candidates inside LKH) similarly improves LKH at equal time — [arXiv 2110.07983](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.07983)
- Search found no 2025 pure neural solver that clearly beats LKH-3 at 100K nodes on optimality gap; the stronger 2025 claims are hybrids (search synthesis across GLOP, ViTSP, LocalEscaper and FrontierCO, above)

### Inferences
- Where neural methods win: (a) amortized solving of large batches of small, i.i.d. instances on a GPU; (b) fixed, very short time budgets at large n (real-time dispatch), with quality several percent worse; (c) as learned components inside classical solvers (candidate sets, subregion selection, operator choice), where they can beat the classical solver at equal time; (d) quick adaptation to many VRP variants without hand-engineering a heuristic per variant (the multi-task models).
- Where they lose: quality at moderate time against LKH-3 (TSP) and HGS or PyVRP (CVRP and variants); robustness to distribution or size shift and to real road networks; MIS and Max-Cut against greedy or KaMIS-type solvers at matched time. Many reported wins depend on unfair timing (CPU-single-thread baselines vs GPU batch, excluded I/O) or on decoders that do most of the work.
- Recurring evaluation pitfalls: LKH-3 run with too few trials; HGS missing from CVRP tables; per-instance time versus total batch time; heatmap ablations without a non-learned prior; benchmarks limited to uniform random 2D Euclidean instances.

### Gaps
- No single harmonized 2026 leaderboard of neural versus HGS on CVRPLib X instances was found; numbers are spread across papers.
- Scheduling (JSSP/FJSP) results against OR-Tools CP-SAT for L2D successors (2023-2026) were not covered in this session.
- FrontierCO's per-problem numbers were not extracted beyond the abstract.
