How the model reads a pool, and what each panel shows

1. Tokens. Every atom and every localized orbital of the pool is a token. An atom token reads its element and formal charge (embedded) and its continuous descriptors (Mulliken charge, f+, f−, dual descriptor, frontier weights, %Vbur, chirality). An orbital token reads only continuous descriptors (occupation, centres and their weights, s/p/d shares, alignment, overlap population, distance, radius) and its spectral set. The σ / π / n labels on the page are the classifier's diagnostics, shown to name the orbitals; the model never reads them.

2. h(0). The first vector of a token is a sum: E_Z + E_charge + MLP_A(inputs) for an atom; MLP_O(inputs) + the spectral set encoder + the centre-weighted atom vectors it inherits for an orbital; plus the fragment embedding. The learned MECH token, at position 0, will collect what the decoder needs to choose the step. The token panel shows each part's norm and its alignment with h(0).

3. Layers. Each layer is attention plus a feed-forward block. A logit is content (q·k/√d) plus a learned bias from the pair inputs: the pair track (dense), a relative bias of distance, bond order, graph distance, Wiberg index and the orbital-pair physics z (flex), or the pair MLP on the keys a learned indexer selects (sparse). Colour the map by attention, by cosine similarity to the selected token, or by how far each token moved from h(0). The heads table compares the spread of the bias logits with the spread of the content logits: a head with a large bias share follows the pair inputs more than what the tokens carry.

4. Readout. The decoder scores every candidate anchor (a bond that forms or breaks) as site terms (both atoms' final vectors against the step query read from MECH) + a pair term (the atom-pair vector) + a physics term (MLP of the pooled orbital-pair features y_ij, with the gap factor G). Given the top anchor, it fills in the rest of R greedily.

Every layer was rebuilt from its recorded inputs and checked against the model's own output; the largest deviation is in the header.
