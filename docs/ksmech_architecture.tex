\begin{abstract}
We specify a deep-learning model that predicts organic reaction mechanisms as sequences of elementary steps. Each step is predicted as the change it makes to the Lewis structure, the reaction matrix of Dugundji and Ugi: which bonds form and break and where electron pairs end up. Curved arrows are not predicted; they are one reading of that change, reconstructed afterwards together with the localized orbitals whose interaction drives it. Molecules are featurized with the GFN2-xTB tight-binding Hamiltonian and represented as two token families, atoms and localized orbitals; an orbital token carries only continuous physical descriptors and its spectral decomposition over the canonical orbitals, never a chemist's label such as $\sigma$ or $\pi$. A two-track transformer (a token track and a pair track) encodes the system. The pair track carries the orbital-interaction quantities of the Klopman--Salem perturbation equation that need no geometry of approach: the spectral gap factor of the filled--empty term, computed from the canonical spectra, and, within a molecule, the coupling and alignment of two orbitals in the reactant pose. They reach every attention head as a learned bias. An edit decoder predicts the reaction matrix, anchored on a bond that forms, with the same pair quantities pooled onto atom pairs; the matrix is added to the bond--electron matrix, the intermediate is re-featurized, and the loop continues until a stop token.

The document is in three parts. Part~I sets out the physical theory the model is built on: the tight-binding electronic-structure problem, orbital localization, second-order perturbation theory of intermolecular interaction, conceptual density-functional descriptors, and the bond--electron matrix formalism of curved arrows. Part~II gives the minimum of deep-learning theory needed to read the architecture. Part~III is the specification proper. Appendices fix the derivations and numerical techniques.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope of the physical model}
\label{sec:scope}
The quantity the model must predict is \emph{how the Lewis structure changes} in an elementary step: which bonds form and break, and where electron pairs end up. What drives that change is the interaction between the filled levels of one site and the empty levels of another, and the physical theory that speaks about exactly this is molecular-orbital perturbation theory: two closed-shell fragments approach, their orbitals begin to overlap, and the energy change to second order is a sum over pairs of a filled orbital on one fragment and an empty orbital on the other. Everything in Part~I serves that one equation. We need (i) orbitals and their energies for a molecule of any size and charge, cheaply (Section~\ref{sec:es}); (ii) a way to make those orbitals local, so that the interaction of two of them can be attributed to the atoms that carry them (Section~\ref{sec:loc}); (iii) the perturbation expression itself and the approximations that make it evaluable from the quantities we have (Section~\ref{sec:pt}); (iv) atom-resolved reactivity descriptors that complement it (Section~\ref{sec:cdft}); and (v) the bookkeeping formalism in which an elementary step is written (Section~\ref{sec:be}).

\section{Electronic structure in a finite basis}
\label{sec:es}

\subsection{The Roothaan problem}
Within the Born--Oppenheimer approximation the electronic ground state of a closed-shell molecule with fixed nuclei is approximated by a single Slater determinant of doubly occupied molecular orbitals (MOs) $\psi_k$. Expanding each MO in a finite set of atom-centred basis functions (atomic orbitals, AOs) $\chi_\mu$,
\begin{equation}
\psi_k(\vb{r})=\sum_{\mu} C_{\mu k}\,\chi_\mu(\vb{r}),
\end{equation}
the variational condition becomes the generalized eigenvalue problem
\begin{equation}
\vb{F}\vb{C}=\vb{S}\vb{C}\vb{\epsilon},\qquad S_{\mu\nu}=\langle\chi_\mu|\chi_\nu\rangle,\qquad \vb{C}^{\!\top}\vb{S}\vb{C}=\vb{I},
\label{eq:roothaan}
\end{equation}
where $\vb{F}$ is the Fock matrix, the one-electron effective Hamiltonian that depends on the occupied orbitals through the density matrix $\vb{P}=2\vb{C}_{\occ}\vb{C}_{\occ}^{\!\top}$, so that \eqref{eq:roothaan} is solved self-consistently (SCF). The eigenvectors $\vb{C}$ are the \emph{canonical} MOs and the eigenvalues $\epsilon_k$ their orbital energies. Two properties of the converged solution are used throughout:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Koopmans' theorem.} $-\epsilon_{\mathrm{HOMO}}$ approximates the ionization energy and $-\epsilon_{\mathrm{LUMO}}$ the electron affinity, so orbital energies are the natural scale for donor and acceptor strength.
\item \textbf{Brillouin's condition.} At convergence the Fock matrix has no matrix elements between occupied and virtual canonical orbitals, $F_{kv}=0$ for $k\in\occ$, $v\in\virt$. The occupied and virtual spaces of a converged molecule are already fully mixed; nothing within one molecule remains to be ``perturbed'' at first order. This is why the intermolecular perturbation theory of Section~\ref{sec:pt} applies to \emph{separate} fragments, and why its evaluation inside a converged molecule is identically zero (Section~\ref{sec:contact-theory}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The GFN2-xTB tight-binding Hamiltonian}
Solving \eqref{eq:roothaan} with a first-principles Fock operator is too expensive for tens of thousands of molecules and all their intermediates. Semi-empirical tight-binding methods keep the structure of \eqref{eq:roothaan}, a minimal valence basis, and an explicit overlap matrix, but build $\vb{F}$ from parametrized atomic quantities. GFN2-xTB \cite{gfn2} is the method used here. Its total energy is
\begin{equation}
E=E_{\mathrm{rep}}+E_{\mathrm{EHT}}+E_{\mathrm{IES+IXC}}+E_{\mathrm{AES}}+E_{\mathrm{AXC}}+E_{\mathrm{disp}}+E_{\mathrm{solv}},
\end{equation}
with a pairwise repulsion, an extended-H\"uckel-type band energy, isotropic and anisotropic (multipole) electrostatics and exchange--correlation terms expanded to second (and partly third) order in atomic charge fluctuations, a D4 dispersion correction, and an implicit-solvation term (ALPB \cite{alpb}, used here with water parameters). The zeroth-order Hamiltonian has the extended-H\"uckel form
\begin{equation}
H^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12\,K_{\ell\ell'}\,\big(h_\mu+h_\nu\big)\,S_{\mu\nu}\,\Pi(R_{AB})\,X(\mathrm{EN}_A,\mathrm{EN}_B),
\label{eq:eht}
\end{equation}
where $h_\mu$ are atomic-shell energy levels, $K_{\ell\ell'}$ shell-pair scaling constants, and $\Pi$, $X$ distance- and electronegativity-dependent scaling functions; the charge-dependent terms add diagonal and off-diagonal shifts that depend on the Mulliken charges and are iterated to self-consistency. The basis consists of Slater-type orbitals approximated by contracted Gaussians (STO-$n$G): one $1s$ function on hydrogen, $ns$ and $np$ on main-group elements, and an added polarization $d$ shell on heavier $p$-block elements (S, P, Cl, Br, I among them). Because the basis is minimal and valence-only, the number of MOs equals the number of valence AOs, there are no core orbitals, and every virtual orbital has a simple interpretation as the antibonding or empty counterpart of an occupied one.

What the method exports at convergence, and what the model uses, is: $\vb{S}$, $\vb{F}$, $\vb{C}$, $\vb{\epsilon}$, the occupations $n_k$, the atomic partial charges, and the AO-to-atom map with each AO's angular momentum. The structure of \eqref{eq:eht} also justifies the Wolfsberg--Helmholz approximation used for resonance integrals in Section~\ref{sec:pt}: in a tight-binding Hamiltonian the off-diagonal element between two AOs is, to leading order, proportional to their overlap times their mean diagonal energy.

\subsection{Open shells and fractional occupation}
For radicals the $\alpha$ and $\beta$ spin orbitals are solved separately (unrestricted formalism); the singly occupied orbital is the $\alpha$ orbital with no $\beta$ partner. GFN2-xTB applies a finite electronic temperature (Fermi smearing) to aid convergence; for molecules with a vanishing HOMO--LUMO gap this leaves fractional occupations $0<n_k<2$. Such molecules are outside the closed-shell perturbative picture and are flagged rather than forced.

\subsection{Population analysis}
\label{sec:mulliken}
The Mulliken partition assigns the electron density to atoms through the AO basis. The gross population of AO $\mu$ is $(\vb{P}\vb{S})_{\mu\mu}$, and the partial charge of atom $A$ is
\begin{equation}
q_A=Z^{\mathrm{val}}_A-\sum_{\mu\in A}(\vb{P}\vb{S})_{\mu\mu}.
\end{equation}
The same partition applied to a single orbital $\psi_k$ gives its \emph{population on atom $A$},
\begin{equation}
Q^{(k)}_A=\sum_{\mu\in A}\sum_\nu C_{\mu k}S_{\mu\nu}C_{\nu k},\qquad \sum_A Q^{(k)}_A=1,
\label{eq:orbpop}
\end{equation}
and between two atoms the \emph{overlap population}
\begin{equation}
\Pi^{(k)}_{AB}=2\sum_{\mu\in A}\sum_{\nu\in B}C_{\mu k}S_{\mu\nu}C_{\nu k},
\label{eq:overlappop}
\end{equation}
whose sign tells whether the orbital is bonding ($\Pi>0$) or antibonding ($\Pi<0$) between $A$ and $B$. Equations~\eqref{eq:orbpop} and \eqref{eq:overlappop} are the only tools used to describe what an orbital \emph{is}: where it sits, how it is shared, and whether it binds. They are basis-dependent, which is acceptable because the basis is fixed for the whole project.

\section{Localized molecular orbitals}
\label{sec:loc}

\subsection{Invariance and the freedom it leaves}
A single-determinant wavefunction is invariant under any unitary rotation of its occupied orbitals among themselves: for $\vb{L}_{\occ}=\vb{C}_{\occ}\vb{U}$ with $\vb{U}$ orthogonal, the determinant, the density matrix, the energy and all observables are unchanged. The same holds separately for the virtual space. The canonical MOs are one choice inside this freedom (the one that diagonalizes $\vb{F}$); they are typically delocalized over the whole molecule. Curved arrows, however, speak of \emph{this} lone pair and \emph{that} $\sigma$ bond. Localization chooses, inside the same subspace, the rotation that makes each orbital as atom-local as possible. It is a change of basis, not an approximation and not a chemical assumption: the set of localized orbitals spans exactly the occupied (or virtual) space.

\subsection{Population-based localization: Pipek--Mezey and intrinsic bond orbitals}
Pipek and Mezey \cite{pm} define locality through atomic populations: maximize
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{P}_p(\vb{U})=\sum_{r}\sum_{A}\big(Q^{(r)}_A\big)^p,\qquad p=2,
\label{eq:pm}
\end{equation}
the sum over orbitals of the atomic populations raised to a power. $\mathcal{P}$ is largest when each orbital's population is concentrated on as few atoms as possible (one atom for a lone pair, two for a bond). Unlike Foster--Boys localization it does not mix $\sigma$ and $\pi$ into ``banana'' bonds, which is why it is the natural choice when the descriptors must separate in-plane from out-of-plane character; and it needs only $\vb{S}$, not dipole integrals. The maximum is found by successive $2\times2$ Jacobi rotations (Appendix~\ref{app:pm}).

With Mulliken populations and $p=2$ the method has a known weakness that matters here: inside the $\pi$ space of an aromatic ring the functional is nearly flat, Kekul\'e-like and delocalized solutions have almost the same value, the sweeps stall against the iteration cap, and the returned orbitals are an arbitrary point on a plateau. Knizia's \emph{intrinsic bond orbitals} (IBO) \cite{ibo} fix this with two changes: the populations are those of intrinsic atomic orbitals (IAO), which are free of the pathologies of Mulliken charges, and the exponent is $p=4$, which breaks the near-degeneracy and strongly prefers the localized solution. Benzene then converges in a few tens of sweeps to three two-centre $\pi$ bonds. In a minimal valence basis such as GFN2-xTB's the IAO construction reduces to the symmetrically orthogonalized AOs, $\vb{S}^{1/2}\vb{C}$, so the IAO populations are L\"owdin populations and the method needs no reference basis. The model uses IBO ($p=4$, L\"owdin populations) on the occupied, virtual and singly occupied blocks separately, started from a pivoted Cholesky decomposition of each block's density matrix, which is itself localized and non-iterative \cite{cholesky}. Pipek--Mezey with Mulliken populations is retained only as a comparison.

Two features of the solution matter for the model:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Where the subspace has a strongly preferred local basis ($\sigma$ bonds, lone pairs, isolated $\pi$ bonds) the maximum is sharp and unique up to permutation.
\item In an aromatic ring the localized solution is one of two equivalent Kekul\'e structures; the deterministic start selects one. This symmetry breaking is harmless for the model: labels are aligned by atom (every token with weight on the site), tokens carry no name, and the spectral decomposition of the next section makes every energy-dependent quantity invariant to the choice.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Spectral decomposition of a localized orbital}
\label{sec:spectral}
A localized orbital $\vb{l}_r=\sum_k U_{kr}\vb{c}_k$ is not an eigenfunction of $\vb{F}$. Its exact content in terms of eigenfunctions is given by the weights
\begin{equation}
p_{rk}=U_{kr}^2,\qquad \sum_k p_{rk}=1,
\label{eq:spectral-theory}
\end{equation}
so that $\langle\vb{l}_r|\vb{F}|\vb{l}_r\rangle=\sum_k p_{rk}\epsilon_k$ and, more importantly, any second-order expression with an energy denominator can be written exactly as a sum over canonical pairs weighted by $p_{rk}p_{sm}$ (Section~\ref{sec:pt-lmo}). The weights depend on the localization, but the set of canonical energies does not, and sums over a complete localized set reduce to canonical sums. This is what makes an aromatic ring unproblematic: three ring $\pi$ LMOs, whichever Kekul\'e structure the localization selected, carry total weight exactly one on each of the three canonical $\pi$ orbitals.

\section{Perturbation theory of the interaction between two molecules}
\label{sec:pt}

\subsection{Setting}
Let fragments $A$ and $B$, each a converged closed-shell molecule, approach to a geometry where their orbitals overlap weakly. Treat the orbitals of the combined system as linear combinations of the fragment orbitals $\set{\psi_r}$ of $A$ and $\set{\psi_s}$ of $B$, and the interaction through the matrix elements $H_{rs}=\langle\psi_r|\hat H|\psi_s\rangle$ and overlaps $S_{rs}=\langle\psi_r|\psi_s\rangle$, both small. Three kinds of pairs contribute (Appendix~\ref{app:ks} gives the derivation):
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item two \emph{filled} orbitals, one on each fragment: four electrons in two orbitals that mix; the net effect is a destabilization of order $S_{rs}^2$ (closed-shell or ``steric'' repulsion);
\item a \emph{filled} orbital on one fragment and an \emph{empty} one on the other: two electrons in the lower of two mixed orbitals; a stabilization of order $H_{rs}^2/(E_r-E_s)$;
\item the electrostatic interaction of the unperturbed charge distributions, which does not need orbital mixing at all.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Klopman--Salem equation}
\label{sec:ks-theory}
Expanding the fragment MOs in AOs, $\psi_r=\sum_a c_{ra}\chi_a$, writing the AO interaction elements as resonance integrals $\beta_{ab}$ and keeping terms to second order, Klopman \cite{klopman} and Salem \cite{salem} obtained
\begin{equation}
\small
\Delta E
=\underbrace{-\sum_{a,b}\big(q_a+q_b\big)\,\beta_{ab}\,S_{ab}}_{\text{(1) filled--filled repulsion}}
\;+\;\underbrace{\sum_{k<l}\frac{Q_kQ_l}{\varepsilon R_{kl}}}_{\text{(2) Coulomb}}
\;+\;\underbrace{\sum_{r\in\occ(A)}\sum_{s\in\virt(B)}\frac{2\big(\sum_{a,b}c_{ra}\,c_{sb}\,\beta_{ab}\big)^2}{E_r-E_s}
+\big(A\leftrightarrow B\big)}_{\text{(3) filled--empty orbital interaction}} ,
\label{eq:ks-theory}
\end{equation}
where $q_a$ are AO populations of the occupied orbitals, $Q_k$ atomic charges, $R_{kl}$ interatomic distances and $\varepsilon$ a local dielectric constant. Each term has a reading that organic chemists use daily:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Term (1)} is always repulsive and grows with overlap between filled orbitals: it is the quantitative content of steric hindrance.
\item \textbf{Term (2)} favours the approach of oppositely charged atoms irrespective of orbitals: \emph{charge control}. It dominates when the gap $|E_r-E_s|$ is large (hard reagents).
\item \textbf{Term (3)} is stabilizing and dominated by the pair of orbitals with the smallest gap and the largest coefficients on the atoms that meet: \emph{orbital control}. Restricted to the single pair HOMO($A$)/LUMO($B$) it is Fukui's frontier-orbital theory; it dominates when the gap is small (soft reagents).
\end{itemize}
Klopman's hard/soft classification of nucleophiles and electrophiles is the statement of which of (2) and (3) wins. The equation is pairwise in orbitals, and Term~(3) is pairwise in exactly the objects a curved arrow names: one filled orbital and one empty one. That is the whole reason it is the backbone of this model.

\subsection{The resonance integral}
The AO interaction elements $\beta_{ab}$ between two fragments are not available from either fragment's own calculation. The Wolfsberg--Helmholz approximation \cite{wh}, which is also the structure of the tight-binding Hamiltonian \eqref{eq:eht}, sets
\begin{equation}
\beta_{ab}=K\,S_{ab}\,\tfrac12\big(H_{aa}+H_{bb}\big),\qquad K\approx1.75,
\label{eq:wh-theory}
\end{equation}
so that everything reduces to one overlap integral between the two AOs and their diagonal energies. With the MO coefficients inserted, the coupling in Term~(3) becomes $\sum_{ab}c_{ra}c_{sb}\beta_{ab}\approx K\,\bar F_{rs}\,S_{rs}$ where $S_{rs}$ is the overlap of the two \emph{orbitals} at the geometry of approach. The overlap is the only geometry-dependent factor; the energies and coefficients are intrinsic to each fragment.

\subsection{Evaluation with localized orbitals}
\label{sec:pt-lmo}
The derivation of \eqref{eq:ks-theory} assumes fragment orbitals that are eigenfunctions of their fragment Hamiltonians. For localized orbitals this is not true, but the second-order expression is bilinear in the two orbitals and can be re-expanded. Writing $\vb{l}_r=\sum_k U_{kr}\vb{c}_k$, $\vb{l}_s=\sum_m U_{ms}\vb{c}_m$ and neglecting cross terms between different canonical pairs (which vanish on average under the arbitrary phases of a plateau localization) gives
\begin{equation}
\Delta E^{(3)}_{rs}\approx\big(K\,\bar F_{rs}\,S_{rs}\big)^2\;\sum_{k\in\occ}\sum_{m\in\virt}\frac{2\,p_{rk}\,p_{sm}}{\epsilon_k-\epsilon_m}
\;=:\;\big(K\,\bar F_{rs}\,S_{rs}\big)^2\,G(r,s),
\label{eq:ks3-theory}
\end{equation}
a product of a \emph{coupling} that depends on how the two local lobes overlap, and a \emph{spectral gap factor} $G$ that depends only on canonical energies and localization weights. $G$ is negative, and for one-component orbitals reduces to $2/(\epsilon_{k_0}-\epsilon_{m_0})$.

\subsection{What is defined inside one molecule and what is not}
\label{sec:contact-theory}
Equation~\eqref{eq:ks-theory} describes two systems not yet mixed. Inside a converged molecule, Brillouin's condition makes the occupied--virtual Fock block zero and the localized occupied and virtual orbitals $\vb{S}$-orthogonal, so Term~(3) evaluated with the molecule's own $\vb{S}$ and $\vb{F}$ is identically zero. This is not a statement that intramolecular reactions do not occur; it is a statement that the reactant geometry is not the reactive geometry. A chain that cyclizes must first fold until the two lobes meet, exactly as two separate molecules must diffuse together. In both cases the overlap that decides reactivity is the one \emph{at contact}, in the pose the reaction adopts, and no calculation on the separate reactants provides that pose. Of the factors of \eqref{eq:ks3-theory} the model therefore keeps the one that is intrinsic to the two orbitals, the gap factor $G$, for every pair, intra- and intermolecular. What a molecule does define for its own pairs, the residual coupling already present in the reactant pose and the distance the chain must span, enters as separate descriptors.

\subsection{Why no contact geometry}
\label{sec:beyond}
Equation~\eqref{eq:ks-theory} is an expansion in small overlaps around separated fragments, and it does not say where the fragments meet. Terms~(1) and~(3) are both of second order in the overlap (the repulsion scales as $\beta S\propto S^2$, the stabilization as $\beta^2\propto S^2$), so their ratio has no minimum along the approach coordinate: the contact distance is set by the short-range exchange repulsion that the expansion leaves out. The strain of reaching the reactive pose, the pyramidalization of an attacked carbon, the stretching of a leaving bond and the folding of a chain, is invisible to it as well \cite{bickelhaupt}. A coupling evaluated at a contact geometry is therefore a model of the approach, not a property of the reactants, and the model computes none: it keeps the geometry-free factor $G$ and the reactant-pose descriptors, and leaves the approach to be learned from the token descriptors.

\section{Conceptual density-functional descriptors}
\label{sec:cdft}
Conceptual DFT \cite{parryang} defines reactivity indices as derivatives of the energy with respect to electron number $N$ at fixed external potential. With finite differences over $N\pm1$ and Koopmans' theorem:
\begin{equation}
\mu=\Big(\frac{\partial E}{\partial N}\Big)_v\approx\tfrac12(\epsilon_{\mathrm{HOMO}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{LUMO}}),\qquad
\eta=\Big(\frac{\partial^2E}{\partial N^2}\Big)_v\approx\tfrac12(\epsilon_{\mathrm{LUMO}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{HOMO}}),\qquad
\omega=\frac{\mu^2}{2\eta},
\end{equation}
the chemical potential, the hardness, and the electrophilicity index. The Fukui function $f(\vb{r})=(\partial\rho(\vb{r})/\partial N)_v$ localizes the response; condensed to atoms \cite{yangmortier} it reads
\begin{equation}
\tilde f^{+}_A=q_A(N)-q_A(N{+}1),\qquad \tilde f^{-}_A=q_A(N{-}1)-q_A(N),\qquad \Delta f_A=\tilde f^+_A-\tilde f^-_A,
\end{equation}
for nucleophilic attack on $A$, electrophilic attack on $A$, and the dual descriptor \cite{morell} whose sign separates electrophilic from nucleophilic sites. In the frozen-orbital approximation the added or removed electron occupies the LUMO or HOMO without relaxation, and the condensed Fukui functions become orbital populations,
\begin{equation}
f^{+}_A=Q^{(\mathrm{LUMO})}_A,\qquad f^{-}_A=Q^{(\mathrm{HOMO})}_A,
\end{equation}
with $Q$ from \eqref{eq:orbpop}. The two families answer different questions: the finite-difference form includes the relaxation of the whole density (and in a minimal basis is dominated by it), the frozen-orbital form is the shape of the frontier orbital itself, which is the quantity Term~(3) depends on. Generalizing the latter to the $K$ orbitals nearest the gap gives the per-atom frontier weights $\omega_{A,k}=Q^{(k)}_A$ used as atom descriptors; these carry regiochemical information (ortho/para versus meta) that no single localized orbital can.

\section{Curved arrows as bond--electron matrix edits}
\label{sec:be}
Dugundji and Ugi \cite{ugi} represent a Lewis structure of $n$ atoms by the symmetric \emph{bond--electron matrix} $\vb{B}\in\mathbb{N}^{n\times n}$: $B_{ij}$ ($i\ne j$) is the formal bond order between atoms $i$ and $j$, and $B_{ii}$ the number of free (non-bonding) valence electrons on atom $i$. The total valence-electron count is $\sum_i B_{ii}+\sum_{i\ne j}B_{ij}$, and the formal charge of atom $i$ is $Z^{\mathrm{val}}_i-B_{ii}-\sum_{j\ne i}B_{ij}$. A reaction is a \emph{reaction matrix} $\vb{R}=\vb{B}_{\mathrm{prod}}-\vb{B}_{\mathrm{reac}}$ with integer entries; electron conservation is $\sum_{ij}R_{ij}=0$. A curved arrow is the smallest such edit:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item lone pair on $i$ $\to$ atom $j$: $R_{ii}=-2$, $R_{ij}=R_{ji}=+1$;
\item bond $(i,k)$ $\to$ atom $j$ with the new bond at $i$: $R_{ik}=R_{ki}=-1$, $R_{ij}=R_{ji}=+1$;
\item bond $(i,k)$ $\to$ lone pair on $k$ (a leaving group): $R_{ik}=R_{ki}=-1$, $R_{kk}=+2$.
\end{itemize}
An elementary step is the sum of its arrows' matrices applied at once; formal charges follow from the definition and need no separate rule.

The model predicts $\vb{R}$ itself, not the arrows, for three reasons. First, the decomposition of $\vb{R}$ into arrows is not unique, and the ambiguity is physical, not notational. When a bond is ionic in the wavefunction (an alkoxide's O--Li, the N--P bond of a Mitsunobu betaine, the C--Cl bond of a tetrahedral intermediate), the pair of that bond and a lone pair of its atom are the same localized orbital, so no orbital-level label can say whether ``the lone pair'' or ``the bond'' moved; $\vb{R}$ is the same either way. Relays (E2, enolate alkylation) and the choice of which ligand leaves a hypervalent centre are further readings of one $\vb{R}$. Second, $\vb{R}$ is what the step does: the product is $\vb{B}_{\mathrm{reac}}+\vb{R}$, mass is conserved by construction, and charge conservation is the single linear constraint $\sum_i R_{ii}+2\sum_{i<j}R_{ij}=0$. Third, $\vb{R}$ is sparse and small: on PMechDB an elementary step changes one to eight bond orders, always by $\pm1$, and the non-bonding electrons of at most two atoms, by $\pm2$. Arrows remain the reader's language. They are reconstructed from $\vb{R}$ after prediction, together with the orbital pairs whose Klopman--Salem interaction drives the bonds that form (Section~\ref{sec:interpret}).

Resonance structures are distinct $\vb{B}$ matrices of the same molecule; the quantum-mechanical state is their superposition, which is what the wavefunction of Section~\ref{sec:es} already represents. Two reaction matrices that lead to the same resonance hybrid describe the same step. Among them the physically meaningful ones are the \emph{parsimonious} ones: those that reach the product's dominant resonance contributor (the fewest formal charges) with the fewest changed entries (Section~\ref{sec:labels}). Appendix~\ref{app:be} gives a worked example.

\section{Steric descriptors}
The repulsive Term~(1) of \eqref{eq:ks-theory} needs the overlap of filled orbitals at the reactive geometry, which no fragment calculation provides. The percent buried volume $V^{\mathrm{bur}}_A$ \cite{vbur}, the fraction of a sphere of radius $3.5$~\AA\ centred on atom $A$ that is occupied by the van der Waals volume of the rest of the molecule, is used as a geometric proxy for how much filled electron density surrounds a site. It is an input descriptor, not a prior on reactivity.

\section{What is being learned}
\label{sec:learn}
The model is a conditional probability distribution $P_\theta(\vb{R}_t\mid\mathcal{M}_{t-1})$ over elementary steps given the current molecules, with parameters $\theta$ fitted by maximizing the log-likelihood of annotated steps. An elementary step is a sparse integer matrix over the atoms present, so the problem is a \emph{structured classification over sets whose size varies with the molecule}: one choice from the candidate atom pairs (a bond order that rises), then a value for every other entry given that choice. The per-choice loss is the cross-entropy $-\log P_\theta(\text{label})$. When several labels are physically equivalent (two resonance-equivalent ways of writing the same step, two bonds that form in the same step) the label is a set $\mathcal{D}$ and the loss is the negative log of the total probability mass on the set, $-\log\sum_{x\in\mathcal{D}}P_\theta(x)$: the model is rewarded for putting probability on any equivalent choice and never forced to prefer one.

\section{Attention and the transformer}
\label{sec:attn}
A transformer \cite{vaswani} maps a set of $N$ input vectors $\set{\vb{h}_i}$ to a set of $N$ output vectors by stacking layers of \emph{attention}. In one head, each token forms a query $\vb{q}_i=\vb{W}_Q\vb{h}_i$, a key $\vb{k}_j=\vb{W}_K\vb{h}_j$ and a value $\vb{v}_j=\vb{W}_V\vb{h}_j$; the output for token $i$ is a weighted average of all values,
\begin{equation}
\vb{a}_i=\sum_j \alpha_{ij}\vb{v}_j,\qquad \alpha_{ij}=\softmax_j(\ell_{ij}),\qquad \ell_{ij}=\frac{\vb{q}_i^{\!\top}\vb{k}_j}{\sqrt{d}} ,
\label{eq:attn}
\end{equation}
where the \emph{logits} $\ell_{ij}$ measure how relevant token $j$ is to token $i$ and the softmax turns them into a distribution. Several heads with independent projections run in parallel and are concatenated; a residual connection, layer normalization and a small position-wise feed-forward network complete the layer. Because \eqref{eq:attn} treats the input as an unordered set, the architecture is permutation-equivariant, which is the right symmetry for atoms and orbitals: nothing depends on the order in which they are listed.

\subsection{Attention bias and the pair track}
The logit $\ell_{ij}$ in \eqref{eq:attn} depends on $i$ and $j$ only through their separate vectors. When something is known about the \emph{pair} (a distance, whether two atoms are bonded, a physical interaction energy) it can be added directly to the logit,
\begin{equation}
\ell_{ij}=\frac{\vb{q}_i^{\!\top}\vb{k}_j}{\sqrt{d}}+b_{ij},
\end{equation}
an \emph{attention bias}. This is how molecular transformers encode geometry: Graphormer \cite{graphormer} adds biases from graph distance and edges, AlphaFold's Evoformer \cite{alphafold} maintains a full pair representation $\vb{p}_{ij}$ that is updated layer by layer and projected into a bias for every head, Uni-Mol \cite{unimol} does the same with 3D distances. The design in Part~III follows the two-track pattern (token vectors $\vb{h}_i$ and pair vectors $\vb{p}_{ij}$): the pair vectors are initialized from the geometry-free orbital-pair quantities of Section~\ref{sec:ks} and projected into a bias for every head. Every head can thus attend by those physical quantities from the first layer on; how much weight each one gets is learned.

\subsection{Invariance to rigid motion}
A network that consumes raw coordinates learns a function that changes when the molecule is rotated. Using only pairwise distances (expanded in a bank of radial basis functions) as geometric input makes the output exactly invariant to rotations and translations with no equivariant machinery. Reflections must then be handled explicitly, since distances cannot distinguish enantiomers: a signed-volume feature per stereocentre restores chirality.

\subsection{Set encoders}
A variable-length, unordered collection of items (the spectral entries of an orbital) is embedded as $\sum_k\phi(\vb{x}_k)$ with a shared small network $\phi$; any permutation-invariant function of a set can be written this way \cite{deepsets}, so no ordering or padding convention leaks into the representation.

\section{Decoding a step}
\label{sec:decode}
\paragraph{Pointer networks.} When the output is an element of the input set, the natural output layer is a \emph{pointer} \cite{pointer}: a query vector is compared with every candidate's encoded vector and the softmax over candidates is the distribution. The candidate set can have any size, no vocabulary is needed, and a mask can remove invalid candidates by setting their logits to $-\infty$. Here the candidates are atom pairs, scored from the pair track.

\paragraph{Structured output by anchoring.} A step changes several entries at once, and they are not independent: the leaving group goes with the nucleophile that displaces it. The output is therefore factorized around one \emph{anchor} entry. A pointer chooses the anchor, and every other entry is classified given it. The anchor carries the choice that is genuinely multimodal (which site reacts); given it, the remaining entries are close to determined, so the conditional factorization is accurate where an unconditional one would mix incompatible sites.

\paragraph{Constrained beam search.} The $B$ most probable anchors are kept. For each, the most probable completions that satisfy the conservation constraint and every atom's valence are enumerated, ranked by total log-probability and merged when they give the same product. This yields a ranked list of distinct steps.

\section{Ablation as method}
Every design choice that is claimed to matter is paired with a configuration flag that removes it, and the model is retrained with and without. The quantity of interest is the difference in held-out accuracy, with several random seeds so the difference can be compared to its own noise. In particular the physical pair features are tested by removing the gap factor from the pair track, by replacing the orbital tokens with atom tokens alone, and by comparing the trained model with the ranking that the untrained gap factor alone produces (the ``physics floor'').

\section{Problem statement}

\subsection{Task}
The state of a reacting system is the \emph{pool} $\mathcal{M}$: the multiset of all molecular species present, reactants, intermediates, by-products and spectators, each with its multiplicity, written as one bond--electron matrix $\vb{B}$ over all its atoms. An \emph{elementary step} is a reaction matrix $\vb{R}$ (Section~\ref{sec:be}) that passes through a single transition state and maps one pool to the next, $\vb{B}'=\vb{B}+\vb{R}$. Its entries are the change of each bond order, $R_{ij}\in\set{-1,0,+1}$ for $i\ne j$, and of each atom's non-bonding electrons, $R_{ii}\in\set{-2,0,+2}$; formal charges follow. Curved arrows and the localized orbitals that interact are not part of the target: they are a reading of $\vb{R}$, reconstructed after prediction (Section~\ref{sec:interpret}).

A \emph{mechanism} is a reaction network: a directed graph whose nodes are pool states and whose edges are elementary steps. It is not a chain. Two steps that touch disjoint atoms are independent and may occur in either order; a species formed late in one turnover (a regenerated catalyst, a by-product acid) can enter an early step of the next; competing steps from the same pool branch. A \emph{pathway} is one path through the network, and an annotated mechanism in the datasets is one such path in one arbitrary order of its independent steps.

The model is therefore a \emph{policy}: a conditional distribution $P_\theta(\vb{R}\mid\mathcal{M})$ over elementary steps given the current pool. Because every species present is in the pool, a step can involve anything formed earlier; because the policy is defined on any pool, it defines the whole network, not one trajectory. The model predicts only the change; it never writes a molecule from scratch, because the product is $\vb{B}+\vb{R}$.

Three consequences follow for data, inference and evaluation. (i)~Training: from an annotated pathway the dependency DAG of its steps is derived (step $j$ depends on $i$ if it consumes a product of $i$ or touches an atom $i$ changed); every topological order is a valid trajectory and all are used as training sequences, so the model does not learn an arbitrary order as if it were chemistry. (ii)~Inference: the search is over pool states keyed by the multiset of sigma keys (Section~\ref{sec:resonance}), beams reaching the same pool are merged, and a pool that recurs up to the stoichiometric products closes a cycle (catalyst turnover); the output is the network with step probabilities on its edges. (iii)~Evaluation at pathway level compares the \emph{set} of steps and the final pool, never the sequence. PMechDB records isolated steps, with no pathway, step index or mechanism class. On it the model is a single-step policy $P_\theta(\vb{R}\mid\mathcal{M})$; the stop head (Section~\ref{sec:decoder}) and every pathway-level quantity need a source with pathways.

\subsection{Why orbitals}
In curved-arrow notation every arrow already starts at a filled orbital and ends at an empty one, and Term~(3) of the Klopman--Salem equation scores exactly such filled--empty pairs. Localized orbitals are therefore the representation in which the physics is evaluated: the model encodes them as tokens, the geometry-free quantities of their interaction (the gap factor of Term~(3) above all) are features of the pair track, and the same quantities pooled over the orbitals two atoms carry inform the bond that forms between them (Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gatom}). The prediction itself is made on atoms, because the change of a Lewis structure is defined on atoms and is the same whichever localized orbitals one attributes it to.

\subsection{Scope}
Polar (two-electron) mechanisms are the primary target. Radical steps (one-electron arrows) are supported by singly occupied orbital tokens that may act as donor or acceptor; they are a later extension and do not change the architecture.

\section{Featurization}
\label{sec:feat}

\subsection{Geometry}
Each molecule is embedded with RDKit ETKDGv3 (explicit hydrogens, MMFF pre-relaxation, RMSD pruning, three candidates), then optimized at the GFN2-xTB level with ALPB implicit water solvation. The lowest-energy xTB conformer is kept. A geometry is accepted only if every input bond $(a,b)$ satisfies $\|\vb{x}_a-\vb{x}_b\|\le 1.3\,(R^{\mathrm{cov}}_a+R^{\mathrm{cov}}_b)$; new contacts are allowed and bonds to Li, Na, K, Mg are not checked. Geometries that break a bond are re-optimized with a flat-bottom harmonic wall on every input bond (penalty beyond $1.2\times$ the covalent-radii sum) and flagged \texttt{RESTRAINED\_GEOM}. Atom order is preserved so that atom-map numbers from the dataset survive.

\subsection{Electronic structure}
A GFN2-xTB single point at the final geometry provides $\vb{S}$, $\vb{F}$, $\vb{C}$, $\epsilon_k$, occupations, Mulliken charges $q_a$, and the AO-to-atom map with per-AO angular momentum. The total charge is the sum of RDKit formal charges; the number of unpaired electrons is the number of RDKit radical electrons.

\subsection{Conceptual-DFT descriptors}
Global descriptors $\mu,\eta,\omega$ and the two Fukui families of Section~\ref{sec:cdft} are stored per molecule and per atom. The frozen-orbital $f^\pm_a$ and the per-atom frontier weights $\omega_{a,k}$ for the four orbitals nearest the gap, HOMO$-1$ to LUMO$+1$ (degenerate blocks within $0.05$~eV summed), are model inputs; the finite-difference $\tilde f^\pm_a,\Delta f_a$ are secondary descriptors.

\subsection{Sterics}
Per-atom buried volume $V^{\mathrm{bur}}_a$ (sphere radius $3.5$~\AA, hydrogens included) is computed on the optimized geometry.

\section{Localized orbital tokens}
\label{sec:tokens}

\subsection{Localization}
Intrinsic-bond-orbital localization (Section~\ref{sec:loc}, Appendix~\ref{app:pm}: L\"owdin populations, exponent 4) is applied separately to the occupied valence block and to the virtual block of $\vb{C}$, by Jacobi $2\times2$ rotations swept until the change in $\mathcal{P}_4$ is below $10^{-8}$ or $200$ sweeps, started deterministically from a pivoted Cholesky factorization of each block's density matrix so that a given QM record always yields the same LMOs. Singly occupied orbitals (open-shell species) form their own sub-block and are localized separately, so that a radical centre yields one token with occupation $1$ rather than contaminating the doubly occupied and virtual blocks. Fractional occupations from Fermi smearing in vanishing-gap species are kept as the continuous occupation descriptor; such molecules are flagged and their partially occupied tokens are excluded from the candidate sets. Because $\vb{U}$ is orthogonal, the LMOs remain $\vb{S}$-orthonormal and span exactly the occupied (or virtual) space.

\subsection{Spectral decomposition}
Each LMO carries its decomposition over the canonical MOs of its block, Eq.~\eqref{eq:spectral-theory}:
\begin{equation}
p_{rk}=U_{kr}^2,\qquad \sum_k p_{rk}=1,\qquad E_r:=\sum_k p_{rk}\,\epsilon_k .
\label{eq:spectral}
\end{equation}
The full vector $\vb{p}_r$ is used wherever a physical quantity is computed (the gap factor $G$); for the model input, the set $\set{(\epsilon_k,p_{rk}):p_{rk}\ge0.01}$ (at most eight entries, renormalized) is encoded together with the spectral mean, variance and retained fraction. The scalar $E_r$ is a diagnostic.

\subsection{Centres and continuous descriptors}
Let $w^{(r)}_a=Q^{(r)}_a/\sum_{a'}Q^{(r)}_{a'}$ be the normalized population \eqref{eq:orbpop} of LMO $r$ on atom $a$, and $\mathcal{A}_r$ the set of \emph{centres}, the atoms with $w^{(r)}_a\ge\tau_{\mathrm{atom}}$ ($\tau_{\mathrm{atom}}=0.15$), ordered by decreasing weight. The main centre is $m(r)=\arg\max_a w^{(r)}_a$. For the two leading centres $a,b$ let $\hat{\vb{e}}_{ab}$ be the unit vector between them and $\vb{c}^{(r)}_a\in\R^3$ the $p$-coefficient vector of $r$ on $a$. The token is described by continuous quantities only:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item occupation $n_r$ ($2$, $0$, $1$ for singly occupied, fractional when smeared);
\item $|\mathcal{A}_r|$ and the four largest weights $w^{(r)}_a$, zero-padded;
\item per-centre $s$, $p$, $d$ fractions of the orbital's population (four leading centres);
\item $\sigma$-versus-$\pi$ character as a number: $\chi_a=|\cos\angle(\vb{c}^{(r)}_a,\hat{\vb{e}}_{ab})|$ and $\chi_b$;
\item the overlap population $\Pi^{(r)}_{ab}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:overlappop}, whose sign distinguishes bonding from antibonding, and the distance $R_{ab}$;
\item spatial extent: the population-weighted radius of gyration of the centres about the centroid $\bar{\vb{x}}_r=\sum_a w^{(r)}_a\vb{x}_a$;
\item the spectral set of Eq.~\eqref{eq:spectral} with its summary statistics.
\end{itemize}
A categorical label (lone pair, $\sigma$, $\pi$, $\sigma^*$, $\pi^*$, empty $p$) is still computed from these same quantities by a rule-based classifier, but it is marked as not a model input and is used only in unit tests, alignment diagnostics and the user interface. The rationale is that orbital \emph{character} must generalize across elements: a $\sigma^*(\mathrm{C{-}Cl})$, a $\sigma^*(\mathrm{C{-}Br})$ and a $\sigma^*(\mathrm{C{-}I})$ should look to the model like the same kind of acceptor at different energies, and a nitrogen lone pair and a carbanion like the same kind of donor; a label would hide that, the descriptors expose it. Delocalized orbitals need no special treatment: a ring $\pi$ orbital spread over four carbons is simply a token with four centres.

\subsection{Token features}
\begin{definition}[Atom token]
\begin{align*}
\vb{u}_a=\big[&Z_a,\ \text{formal charge},\ \text{chirality sign},\ q_a,\ f^+_a,\ f^-_a,\ \Delta f_a,\\
&\omega_{a,\mathrm{HOMO-1}},\ \omega_{a,\mathrm{HOMO}},\ \omega_{a,\mathrm{LUMO}},\ \omega_{a,\mathrm{LUMO+1}},\ V^{\mathrm{bur}}_a\big],\quad\text{plus the position }\vb{x}_a .
\end{align*}
Hybridization, aromaticity flags and functional-group ids are computed and stored for tests, the interface and ablations, but are not model inputs: they are chemists' taxonomies, derivable from geometry, angular composition and the $\pi$ spectrum, and injecting them would contradict the treatment of orbital tokens.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Orbital token]
\begin{align*}
\vb{v}_r=\big[&n_r,\ |\mathcal{A}_r|,\ (w^{(r)}_a)_{a\in\mathcal{A}_r},\ (s,p,d)\text{-fractions},\ \chi_a,\chi_b,\\
&\Pi^{(r)}_{ab},\ R_{ab},\ R_{\mathrm{gyr}},\ \set{(\epsilon_k,p_{rk})}\big],\quad\text{plus }\bar{\vb{x}}_r,\ \mathcal{A}_r,\ \vb{p}_r\text{ and }\vb{l}_r .
\end{align*}
\end{definition}
The coefficient vector $\vb{l}_r$ and the full spectral vector $\vb{p}_r$ are kept on the token only to evaluate the pair features (the gap factor and the cross-centre coupling); they are not embedded directly.

\begin{remark}
The per-atom frontier weights $\omega_{a,k}$ carry exactly the cross-LMO information that a token-level spectral marginal cannot: the ortho/para preference of anisole's HOMO lives in $\omega_{a,\mathrm{HOMO}}$ and reaches the orbital tokens through their centre embeddings (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
\end{remark}

\section{Geometry-free pair features}
\label{sec:ks}
This section specifies the quantities of Section~\ref{sec:pt} that are evaluated for a pair of tokens. Only quantities defined without a geometry of approach are computed (Section~\ref{sec:contact-theory}): the gap factor of the orbital term for every pair, and the coupling and alignment of two orbitals of one molecule in its reactant pose. Pairs are ordered: a donor $r\in\mathcal{O}^{\occ}$ and an acceptor $s\in\mathcal{O}^{\virt}$.

\subsection{Gap factor}
Following Eq.~\eqref{eq:ks3-theory},
\begin{equation}
G(r,s)=\sum_{k\in\occ}\sum_{m\in\virt}\frac{2\,p_{rk}\,p_{sm}}{\epsilon_k-\epsilon_m},
\label{eq:ks3}
\end{equation}
computed from the \emph{full} spectral vectors over the canonical MOs of each block. $G<0$, and a larger $|G|$ means a smaller effective gap. It is the factor of the filled--empty term that depends on the two orbitals alone; the other factor, the coupling at contact, is not computed (Section~\ref{sec:beyond}). The dominant canonical pair $(k,m)$ in $G$ is reported as the physical explanation of a predicted bond.

\subsection{Cross-centre coupling and alignment}
Within one molecule the reactant pose is a geometry that the two orbitals share. With the Wolfsberg--Helmholz resonance integrals of the molecule's own AOs,
\begin{equation}
\beta_{\mu\nu}=K\,S_{\mu\nu}\,\tfrac12\big(F_{\mu\mu}+F_{\nu\nu}\big),\qquad K=1.75,
\end{equation}
the \emph{cross-centre coupling} is $|\vb{l}_r^{\!\top}\vb{B}\,\vb{l}_s|$, with $\vb{B}$ restricted to the AO blocks between the centres of $r$ and the centres of $s$. It is non-zero although the full Term~(3) vanishes inside the molecule (Section~\ref{sec:contact-theory}); it measures pre-organization, and between bonded centres it carries the antiperiplanar requirement of negative hyperconjugation. Its sign is an arbitrary LMO phase, so the magnitude is used. The resonance integral is contracted at the AO level, with the AO diagonal energies $F_{\mu\mu}$, which are negative for every valence shell; the orbital-level shortcut $K\,S_{rs}\,\tfrac12(F_{rr}+F_{ss})$ must not be used, because for an antibonding acceptor $F_{ss}>0$ and the mean cancels spuriously. $K$ is fixed at its textbook value: it only rescales one standardized feature, so a learnable $K$ would carry nothing.

The other intramolecular features are the centroid distance $\|\bar{\vb{x}}_r-\bar{\vb{x}}_s\|$ (how far the chain must fold) and the \emph{stereoelectronic alignment}: the $|\cos|$ of the angle between the $p$ vectors of $r$ and $s$ on their main centres (the lobe axes) and, when the two orbitals sit on adjacent bonds, the cosine of the site--partner--partner--site dihedral ($-1$ is antiperiplanar), the requirement of eliminations and migrations.

\begin{remark}
Pairs are ordered (donor first). The diene$\to$dienophile and dienophile$\to$diene interactions of a Diels--Alder reaction are distinct candidates and both are enumerated.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Pair feature vector}
\label{sec:pairfeat}
For an ordered (occupied $r$, virtual $s$) pair the feature vector $\vb{z}_{rs}$ holds
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item for every pair: $G$, $\log|G|$ and $\mathbb{1}[\phi(r)\ne\phi(s)]$;
\item on the reactant's Lewis structure, between the two centre sets: the bonded flag, the largest bond order, the largest Wiberg bond index of the bonds joining them (from the reactant density in the L\"owdin basis) and the graph distance, capped at $6$; across fragments $0$, $0$, $0$ and $6$;
\item within a fragment: the centroid distance, the cross-centre coupling and the two alignment cosines.
\end{itemize}
An entry that is not defined (an intramolecular feature of a pair across fragments, an alignment without $p$ character on a centre) is zero and masked. All entries are standardized with statistics of the defined entries of the valid pairs of the training set.

The physics enters through the pair features of Section~\ref{sec:ks} alone: there is no contact geometry, no Klopman--Salem attention bias and no frozen head, and all $H$ heads are learned. Version~8.1 of this document specified all three, with a configurational contact free energy for every orbital pair; it is archived with its code on the git branch \texttt{archive/stage-b}. Untrained, those contact terms ranked the first arrow of a step worse than the gap factor alone (PMechDB, molecule split, test top-1: about $3\%$ for the frozen head, $14.3\%$ for $\KS_3+\KS_2$, $20.4\%$ for $G$).

\subsection{Token embeddings}
\paragraph{Atom tokens.}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\vb{h}^{(0)}_a={}&\vb{E}_Z[Z_a]+\vb{E}_{\mathrm{chg}}[\text{formal charge}]\\
&+\MLP_A\big([q_a,\ f^+_a,\ f^-_a,\ \Delta f_a,\ \omega_{a,\mathrm{HOMO-1..LUMO+1}},\ V^{\mathrm{bur}}_a,\ \text{chirality}]\big).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Orbital tokens.}
\begin{equation}
\vb{h}^{(0)}_r=\MLP_O\big(\vb{v}^{\mathrm{cont}}_r\big)+\sum_{k}\MLP_{\Sigma}\big([\epsilon_k,\ p_{rk}]\big)+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_r} w^{(r)}_a\,\vb{h}^{(0)}_a ,
\end{equation}
where $\vb{v}^{\mathrm{cont}}_r$ collects the continuous descriptors of Section~\ref{sec:tokens}, the second term is a set encoder (Section~\ref{sec:attn}) over the spectral entries, and the last term lets an orbital inherit its chemical environment from the atoms that carry it. No categorical orbital type is embedded; a unit test asserts that no model input tensor contains one. Every token also receives a fragment embedding $\vb{E}_{\mathrm{frag}}[\phi(i)\bmod 8]$, and one learned summary token $\vb{h}_{\mathrm{MECH}}$ is prepended. No absolute coordinates are embedded: geometry enters only through pairwise distances, so the model is invariant to rotations and translations by construction. Chirality is preserved through the per-atom signed-volume feature.

\subsection{Pair track initialization}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\vb{p}^{(0)}_{ij}=\MLP_P\Big(\big[&\RBF(\tilde R_{ij})\odot\mathbb{1}[\phi(i)=\phi(j)],\ \vb{z}_{ij},\ \mathbb{1}[\phi(i)=\phi(j)],\\
&\mathbb{1}[\text{bonded}],\ \text{bond order},\ \min(d^{\mathrm{graph}}_{ij},6)\big]\Big),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
with $\RBF$ a bank of 32 Gaussians centred on $[0,8]$~\AA\ and $\vb{z}_{ij}$ the pair feature vector of Section~\ref{sec:pairfeat} for an (occupied, virtual) orbital pair, zero and masked for every other pair. The distance channels are masked to zero for intermolecular pairs, which have no distance in the reactant pose. The distances run between atom positions and orbital centroids. $\vb{z}$ enters with its defined mask on both cells of an orbital pair, $(r,s)$ and $(s,r)$, with an orientation channel ($+1$ from the occupied token, $-1$ back), so a virtual token's attention also sees its donors. Two more channel groups complete the input: the centre weight $w^{(r)}_a$ on the cells between an orbital and its atoms, and a one-hot pair kind (atom--atom, atom--orbital, orbital--atom, orbital--orbital, MECH). The bonded flag, bond order and graph distance are set on atom pairs; for orbital pairs they are the entries of $\vb{z}$. The continuous token inputs are standardized with training-set statistics. A missing atom descriptor is $0$ with a flag of its own.

\subsection{Encoder layer}
Each of the $L=8$ layers performs, for $h=1,\dots,H$,
\begin{align}
\vb{q}^{(h)}_i&=\vb{W}^{(h)}_Q\LN(\vb{h}_i),\quad \vb{k}^{(h)}_j=\vb{W}^{(h)}_K\LN(\vb{h}_j),\quad \vb{v}^{(h)}_j=\vb{W}^{(h)}_V\LN(\vb{h}_j),\\
\ell^{(h)}_{ij}&=\frac{\vb{q}^{(h)\top}_i\vb{k}^{(h)}_j}{\sqrt{d_h}}
+\vb{w}_h^{\!\top}\vb{p}_{ij}
+ m_{ij},
\label{eq:logit}\\
\vb{a}^{(h)}_i&=\sum_j \softmax_j\big(\ell^{(h)}_{ij}\big)\,\vb{v}^{(h)}_j ,
\end{align}
where $\vb{w}_h^{\!\top}\vb{p}_{ij}$ is the pair bias through which each head sees the pair features, and $m_{ij}\in\set{0,-\infty}$ is the padding mask. The head outputs are concatenated, projected, and added residually; a position-wise feed-forward block (width $4d$, GELU) follows. The pair track is then updated in $O(N^2)$,
\begin{equation}
\vb{p}_{ij}\leftarrow\vb{p}_{ij}+\MLP_U\big([\vb{p}_{ij},\ \vb{h}_i,\ \vb{h}_j]\big),
\end{equation}
so later layers see pair features that have absorbed context from both endpoints.

\section{Edit decoder}
\label{sec:decoder}
Let $\vb{h}_i^{(L)}$ be the final token vectors, $\vb{p}^{(L)}_{ij}$ the final pair vectors (the pair track covers atom tokens as well as orbital tokens), and $\vb{s}=\MLP_S(\vb{h}^{(L)}_{\mathrm{MECH}})$ the step query. No step index enters the query: the state is the pool, and PMechDB has no step indices. The decoder predicts $\vb{R}$ on the atoms of the pool, hydrogens included.

\paragraph{Atom-pair physics.} The pair features are defined on orbital pairs; the decoder works on atom pairs. The physics of the atom pair $(i,j)$ is that of the orbitals the two atoms carry, in both directions of donation. The sums run over the valid pairs $\mathcal{V}$, the (occupied, virtual) pairs the candidate rule admits: a doubly occupied donor, an acceptor that is neither $d$-type nor the antibonding partner of the donor's own bond (Section~\ref{sec:tokens}). The donor's own antibond would otherwise inflate every bonded pair. With the channel weights $\omega^{rs}_{ij}=w^{(r)}_i w^{(s)}_j+w^{(r)}_j w^{(s)}_i$ ($w$ the centre weights), the standardized orbital-pair features are pooled into their weighted mean over the defined entries, $\bar{\vb z}_{ij}=\sum_{(r,s)\in\mathcal{V}}\omega^{rs}_{ij}\,\hat{\vb z}_{rs}\big/\sum_{(r,s)\in\mathcal{V}}\omega^{rs}_{ij}$; with the logarithm of the pooled weight, the pooled gap factor below and the Wiberg bond index of $(i,j)$ in the reactant it forms the atom-pair feature vector $\vb{y}_{ij}$. Its gap component is the \emph{pooled gap factor}
\begin{equation}
G^{\mathrm{atom}}_{ij}=\sum_{(r,s)\in\mathcal{V}}\big(w^{(r)}_i w^{(s)}_j+w^{(r)}_j w^{(s)}_i\big)\,G(r,s),
\label{eq:Gatom}
\end{equation}
the atom-pair analogue of $G$, entered into $\vb{y}_{ij}$ as $\operatorname{asinh}G^{\mathrm{atom}}_{ij}$. The physics floor ranks anchors by $|G^{\mathrm{atom}}_{ij}|$, proposing each pair once with the sign \emph{form} when its order may rise and \emph{break} otherwise (Section~\ref{sec:eval}), and the channel $(r,s)$ with the largest term is the orbital reading of the bond (Section~\ref{sec:interpret}).

\paragraph{Anchor.} Every step raises at least one bond order or, in a heterolysis, lowers one. The anchor is a $+1$ entry: a new bond, or a $\pi$ bond on a bonded pair (an amine closing an iminium on a carbocation forms no new $\sigma$ bond). In a step without one, it is a $-1$ entry. A pointer over candidate pairs $\mathcal{C}$ with a sign $\sigma\in\set{\text{form},\text{break}}$:
\begin{equation}
P(e\mid\cdot)=\softmax_{e=(i,j,\sigma)\in\mathcal{C}}\Big(\vb{s}^{\!\top}\vb{W}_{\sigma}\big[\vb{h}^{(L)}_i+\vb{h}^{(L)}_j\big]+\vb{u}_{\sigma}^{\!\top}\vb{p}^{(L)}_{ij}+\vb{v}_{\sigma}^{\!\top}\MLP_Y(\vb{y}_{ij})\Big),
\end{equation}
where the last term is the learned use of the atom-pair physics. A forming anchor may be any pair of atoms whose bond order stays at most $3$; no distance cut-off is imposed, because new bonds form between molecules (8,904 on PMechDB) and within one at graph distances from 2 to 9 (1,028). A breaking anchor is a bonded pair.

\paragraph{Remaining entries.} Given the anchor $e^\star$ on atoms $(a,b)$, every other pair entry and every atom entry is classified:
\begin{align}
P(R_{ij}\mid e^\star,\cdot)&=\softmax_{v\in\{-1,0,+1\}}\MLP_R\big([\vb{p}^{(L)}_{ij},\ \vb{h}^{(L)}_i+\vb{h}^{(L)}_j,\ \vb{c}_{ij}]\big)_v,\\
P(R_{ii}\mid e^\star,\cdot)&=\softmax_{v\in\{-2,0,+2\}}\MLP_N\big([\vb{h}^{(L)}_i,\ \vb{c}_{ii}]\big)_v,
\end{align}
where $\vb{c}$ carries the anchor: $\vb{h}^{(L)}_a+\vb{h}^{(L)}_b$, the pair vectors that join the entry's atoms to $a$ and $b$, and their graph distances to $a$ and $b$. Masks remove $-1$ on a pair without a bond, $+1$ on a triple bond, and $-2$ on an atom without a lone pair.

\paragraph{Joint probability.} $P(\vb{R}\mid\cdot)=P(e^\star\mid\cdot)\prod_{x\ne e^\star}P(R_x\mid e^\star,\cdot)$. Decoding is the constrained beam search of Section~\ref{sec:decode}. For each of the $B$ best anchors, completions are built from the most probable non-zero entries (at most $8$ bond and $2$ atom changes, the PMechDB maximum); those that violate charge conservation or an atom's valence are discarded; the rest are ranked by $\log P(\vb{R})$ and merged by the sigma key of their product.

\paragraph{Stop head.} A step is one matrix, so there is no per-step stop. After each step a Bernoulli head on $\vb{h}^{(L)}_{\mathrm{MECH}}$ of the re-featurized pool decides \texttt{END\_MECH}. It needs a source with pathways; on PMechDB, whose steps are isolated, it is not trained ($\lambda_{\mathrm{stop}}=0$).

\section{Training objective}
\label{sec:loss}
Each annotated step gives a label set $\mathcal{R}$ of equivalent reaction matrices (Section~\ref{sec:labels}). Each $\vb{R}\in\mathcal{R}$ has a set of admissible anchors $\mathcal{E}(\vb{R})$: its $+1$ entries, or its $-1$ entries when it has none. The loss marginalizes over both (Section~\ref{sec:learn}):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{step}}=-\log\sum_{\vb{R}\in\mathcal{R}}\ \sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}(\vb{R})}P(e\mid\cdot)\prod_{x\ne e}P(R_x\mid e,\cdot),
\end{equation}
where $x$ runs over every candidate pair and atom entry. The total loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{step}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{stop}}\,\mathrm{BCE}_{\texttt{END\_MECH}},\qquad \lambda_{\mathrm{stop}}=0.5\ \text{($0$ on PMechDB)}.
\end{equation}
Most entries are zero in every label. Their terms stay in the likelihood: the model is trained to predict that most of the molecule does not change. Each elementary step is an independent training sample, one per (step, topological order) of its pathway's dependency DAG; pools are featurized offline, so no gradient flows across steps.

\paragraph{Resonance augmentation.} A drawn reactant pool is one of its resonance drawings. In training, each molecule of a sample's reactant pool is drawn as a random valid resonance form (Section~\ref{sec:resonance}), and the label set is recomputed for that drawing: the parsimonious class of Section~\ref{sec:labels} with the drawn form's $\vb{B}'_{\mathrm{reac}}$ in place of $\vb{B}_{\mathrm{reac}}$. The model thus learns the step, not the drawing. The resonance pairs are extra equivalence examples: the two drawings of each pair are one pool, and the model is trained to give them the same prediction.

\section{Labels, interpretation and step application}

\subsection{Edit labels}
\label{sec:labels}
Datasets give arrows as atom-map indices. They are applied once, jointly, as one bond--electron edit of the drawn reactants (Appendix~\ref{app:be}), and the result is checked against the annotated products modulo resonance; this gives $\vb{R}_{\mathrm{data}}$. The label set $\mathcal{R}$ is the parsimonious class of $\vb{R}_{\mathrm{data}}$: every $\vb{R}$ such that $\vb{B}_{\mathrm{reac}}+\vb{R}$ is a resonance form of the annotated product (Section~\ref{sec:resonance}: RDKit's structures with the H counts frozen and odd structures dropped, capped at $256$, $\vb{R}_{\mathrm{data}}$ always included), chosen first by the fewest formal charges in that structure and then by the fewest changed entries. Counting entries alone would prefer charge-separated forms, which a drawn reactant often reaches in fewer changes: a hydrazone written as N$^-$--C=OH$^+$, a carboxylate as O$^+$=C--O$^-$. Members are taken in this order of cost and accepted by the sigma key, the check evaluation uses. Every resonance form of the product has its sigma key, so a member is dropped only when its edit cannot be applied, and $\vb{R}_{\mathrm{data}}$ always passes (on PMechDB no member is dropped). Variants that differ only by resonance shifts far from the reaction centre (Kekul\'e swaps of a distant ring, charge-separated forms) are not parsimonious and never enter the label.

On PMechDB the parsimonious class has one member in $94.5\%$ of steps and two in $5.0\%$; the drawn $\vb{R}_{\mathrm{data}}$ is itself parsimonious in $94.8\%$ of steps, and in the others (drawn with an extra resonance shift, or to a charge-separated product form) it is replaced by its parsimonious equivalents. A step whose two sides have one sigma key with the atom maps kept (its class would contain the empty matrix) is a resonance interconversion and is removed (Section~\ref{sec:resonance}). The arrows are used for nothing else. No orbital alignment is needed to build a label, so a step whose arrows cannot be aligned to orbitals (a lone heterolysis) is still a training sample: $99.6\%$ of the PMechDB training steps have a label; the rest involve a molecule whose GFN2-xTB calculation does not converge (mostly Ti and Sn Lewis-acid adducts).

\subsection{Orbital interpretation}
\label{sec:interpret}
A predicted or labeled $\vb{R}$ is read back as curved arrows and orbitals for the chemist and for the interpretability metrics. The arrows are the decomposition of $\vb{R}$ into electron-pair moves: each forming bond takes its pair from a lone pair of one of its atoms or from a breaking bond at that atom, and each breaking bond ends as a lone pair or in a forming bond; where several decompositions exist, the one whose forming bonds have the largest pooled gap factors $|G^{\mathrm{atom}}|$ is shown. The orbital pair of a forming bond $(i,j)$ is the channel $(r,s)$ with the largest term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gatom}.

For dataset arrows the same attribution is made by atom, never by orbital label, and kept as a diagnostic of the token set. For an arrow whose electrons leave atom $a$ (a lone pair on $a$, or a bond $(a,c)$ whose pair forms the new bond at $a$) and arrive at atom $b$ (an atom, or a bond $(b,d)$ that breaks):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item donor site $a$; donor set $\set{r\in\mathcal{O}^{\occ}:\ w^{(r)}_a\ge\tau_{\mathrm{atom}}}$, restricted to tokens that also have weight on $c$ for a bond source (if that empties the set the restriction is dropped and the arrow flagged);
\item acceptor site $b$; acceptor set $\set{s\in\mathcal{O}^{\virt}:\ w^{(s)}_b\ge\tau_{\mathrm{atom}}}$, restricted to tokens with weight on $d$ for a bond sink, excluding the antibonding partner of the donor's own bond and $d$-dominated polarization orbitals; when the step breaks a bond $(b,x)$, restricted to the antibonding tokens on $(b,x)$ where one exists;
\item a carbanion drawn as a lone pair on an atom that carries no one-centre occupied token (an enolate in its carbanion resonance form) aligns to the multi-centre tokens on that atom;
\item an empty set falls back to the tokens nearest to the atom by centroid distance and is flagged.
\end{itemize}
These sets, and the round trip of the attributed tuples, are diagnostics; they are neither labels nor a gate of the data pipeline. They expose exactly where an orbital-level target would have been ambiguous: on PMechDB the attributed tuples reproduce $99.67\%$ of the aligned steps, and the failures are ionic bonds whose pair is a lone pair of their atom and hyperconjugated $\sigma$ donors.

\subsection{Resonance}
\label{sec:resonance}
Products and labels are compared modulo resonance, and the \emph{sigma key} defines it. The sigma key of a molecule is the map-free canonical SMILES of its $\sigma$ skeleton (every bond single, no aromaticity, no stereochemistry) with every atom's H count frozen and every formal charge and unpaired electron set to zero, written with all H counts and followed by the total charge. Two closed-shell Lewis structures with one sigma key have the same atoms, $\sigma$ bonds, H and total charge, hence the same electron count, and differ only in where the $\pi$ bonds, lone pairs and charges sit: they are resonance forms of one molecule, and two structures with different keys are not. The key needs no enumeration, so it is exact and never truncated; a key taken as the smallest SMILES over a capped RDKit enumeration agreed across the forms of only $59\%$ of sampled PMechDB products. The sigma key is used for the label check, the acceptance of the parsimonious class, step-level exact match, the merging of decoder beams and the pool keys.

A dataset step whose two sides have one sigma key with the atom maps kept changes no atom's $\sigma$ bonds or H: it is a resonance interconversion, leaves the training target, and step indices are renumbered ($482$ of the $13{,}090$ accepted PMechDB steps). Those inside the closed-shell domain are kept as \emph{resonance pairs}, two drawings of one pool (Section~\ref{sec:loss}). A degenerate exchange has the same molecules on both sides, hence one map-free key, but its atoms change partners (a thiol--disulfide exchange, a degenerate cation shift in a symmetric cage): it is a real elementary step and stays in the training target ($3$ on PMechDB).

Where the resonance forms themselves are needed (the label class, input augmentation) they are RDKit's enumeration on a copy whose H counts are frozen, keeping only the structures with the input's H count on every atom, its total charge and its unpaired electrons. Without the freeze RDKit re-derives the implicit H of an unbracketed atom in each structure (a nitro O becomes OH$_2^+$), and it also returns structures with unpaired electrons (an azide as N=N--N with two); neither is a resonance form. The wavefunction averages over the forms (Section~\ref{sec:be}), but the formal charges of the atom tokens and the matrix $\vb{B}$ on which $\vb{R}$ is decoded belong to one drawing.

\subsection{Applying a step}
\label{sec:apply}
A reaction matrix, labeled or predicted, is applied by adding it to $\vb{B}$ on an RDKit molecule with explicit valence checks; the result is sanitized and reduced to its sigma key. Nothing is read in order and nothing is inferred: the leaving bond, the source of a pair and the relay are all entries of $\vb{R}$. Round-trip agreement of the dataset arrows, applied as one edit, with the annotated products is the hard requirement of the data pipeline ($\ge95\%$; $100\%$ on PMechDB).

Given reactant SMILES and their stoichiometry: featurize (cached), encode, and run the constrained beam search of Section~\ref{sec:decoder} with width $B=5$, scoring each step by its joint log-probability. Each step is applied as $\vb{B}+\vb{R}$, the new pool is keyed by the sorted multiset of its sigma keys, beams reaching the same pool key are merged, a pool key that recurs up to the stoichiometric products closes a cycle, the pool is re-featurized, and decoding continues up to $T_{\max}=8$ steps or \texttt{END\_MECH}. The result is a reaction network (nodes: pool states; edges: steps with probabilities); pathways are reported as ranked paths through it. Every returned pathway lists, per arrow, the intermediate SMILES, the step probability, the pooled gap factor of the anchor, the orbital pair that dominates it, and the dominant canonical MO pair $(k,m)$ in its $G$ with their energies. A surrogate graph network can replace the xTB call at inference once trained on the cached records; it falls back to xTB for elements outside its training set.

\section{Complexity, invariances, and defaults}

\paragraph{Complexity.} Every valence AO gives one LMO, and the virtual block is the whole valence virtual space, so a pool has about $3.6$ tokens per atom, atom tokens included: on PMechDB a median of $124$ tokens ($p_{90}$ $287$, $p_{99}$ $483$, at most $1{,}487$) for a median of $34$ and at most $411$ atoms, hydrogens included. The pair track costs $O(N^2 d_p)$ memory per layer, and the dense track of the largest pool has $2.2$ million cells, so batches are formed by a token budget rather than a fixed number of steps. Candidate anchors number $N_A(N_A-1)/2$ per sign, and the conditional entries are as many plus $N_A$.

\paragraph{Invariances.} Rotation and translation invariance hold exactly (distances only). Permutation equivariance over tokens holds by construction of attention. Mirror invariance is deliberately broken by the signed-volume chirality feature.

\section{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:eval}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Edit level}: top-$k$ accuracy of the anchor with set marginalization ($k=1,3,5$), and of the whole reaction matrix up to its parsimonious class; the arrows and orbital pairs of the reading are reported, not scored.
\item \textbf{Step level}: exact match of the intermediate's sigma key at top-$k$.
\item \textbf{Pathway level} (sources with pathways): the annotated set of steps (as pool-key pairs) contained in the predicted network at beam 5, order ignored; final pool recovered at any depth $\le8$; for pathways with independent steps, recovery of at least one valid topological order.
\item \textbf{Validity}: sanitizable steps; conservation of heavy atoms and charge.
\item \textbf{Splits}: by reactant molecule (default, leak-proof), leave-one-mechanism-class-out (sources with classes), scaffold.
\item \textbf{Domain coverage}: steps whose species fall outside the closed-shell domain (open-shell metal adducts the SMILES does not mark, bare-metal catalyst placeholders, vanishing-gap species with smeared occupations, resonance interconversions, molecules whose QM calculation failed) are excluded from the training target and from every metric denominator, and counted by reason code; the covered fraction of each dataset is reported as one number next to every accuracy. Charge errors in the source data are repaired at ingest only under explicit rules (a $-1$ on a four-bonded group-14 atom; an onium-to-metal bond read as dative when the metal is $d^0$ or $d^{10}$) and only when the repair yields an even electron count; every repair is logged.
\item \textbf{Ablations}: atom-only tokens; categorical orbital labels added back as input; hybridization, aromaticity and functional-group ids added back as input (both measure what the physical descriptors already carry); $d$-type acceptors allowed; $G$ removed from the pair features; three seeds each.
\item \textbf{References}: the physics floor, the ranking of anchor pairs by the pooled gap factor $|G^{\mathrm{atom}}_{ij}|$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gatom}) with no learned parameters, the atom-pair analogue of ranking orbital pairs by $G$; and a geometry-free LightGBM ranker over the atom-pair candidates (to be built). The existing orbital-pair rankers of a step's first arrow rank another candidate set and are not compared with anchor accuracy.
\item \textbf{Interpretability}: selectivity suite (S$_\mathrm{N}$2 vs.\ E2, 1,2- vs.\ 1,4-addition, hard/soft alkylation, Markovnikov, EAS regiochemistry, Baldwin rules).
\end{itemize}

\section{Design rationale in brief}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \emph{Orbitals as tokens} put the representation in the language of the perturbation theory, \emph{untyped} tokens let orbital character generalize across elements, and \emph{the prediction on atoms} keeps the target free of choices the wavefunction cannot make (a lone pair or an ionic bond's pair, one reading of a relay or another).
\item \emph{A pair track with physical features} gives every head the geometry-free orbital-pair quantities (the gap factor, and the coupling and alignment in the reactant pose) as a learned bias from the first layer, rather than as an auxiliary loss.
\item \emph{Spectral energies} make the gap factor of Term~(3) exact for localized orbitals and invariant to the arbitrary mixture a localization returns inside a flat subspace, which is what makes aromatic and conjugated systems unproblematic.
\item \emph{No contact geometry.} A coupling at the geometry of approach is a model of that approach, not a property of the reactants (Section~\ref{sec:beyond}); the model keeps the factor that is intrinsic to the two orbitals and learns the rest from the token descriptors.
\item \emph{The anchor} resolves where the step happens, which is also the regiochemistry of additions; given it, the rest of the matrix is nearly determined.
\item \emph{The product is $\vb{B}+\vb{R}$}: no molecule is generated, mass is conserved by construction and charge by one linear constraint in decoding.
\item \emph{No chemist's taxonomy as input.} Neither orbital tokens nor atom tokens carry categorical labels ($\sigma/\pi$, hybridization, aromaticity, functional group); everything the model sees is a physical quantity or a Lewis-bookkeeping integer. The physics enters as declared features, each removable by an ablation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The GFN2-xTB basis and exported quantities}
\label{app:gfn2}
Each atomic shell $(n,\ell)$ is represented by one Slater-type orbital $\chi(r)\propto r^{n-1}e^{-\zeta r}Y_{\ell m}$, expanded as a fixed contraction of primitive Gaussians,
\begin{equation}
\chi_\mu(\vb{r})=\sum_{g=1}^{n_g} c_{g}\,N_g\,x^{l}y^{m}z^{n}\,e^{-\alpha_g|\vb{r}-\vb{A}|^2},\qquad l+m+n=\ell,
\end{equation}
with element- and shell-specific exponents $\zeta$ and $n_g$ between 3 and 6 (STO-$n$G). The exponents and contraction coefficients are part of the published parameter set. Shells per element: H $1s$; B--F $2s,2p$; Na--Cl $3s,3p$ with a $3d$ polarization shell on Si, P, S, Cl; similarly for the heavier $p$-block with $d$ on Br and I. Metals carry $s$, $p$ and (for transition metals) $d$ valence shells.

The converged calculation provides: $\vb{S}$ (analytic overlaps in this basis), $\vb{F}$ (the self-consistent Hamiltonian, i.e.\ $\vb{H}^{(0)}$ plus the charge-dependent shifts), $\vb{C}$ and $\vb{\epsilon}$ from \eqref{eq:roothaan}, occupations, Mulliken charges and shell populations, and the atom and angular momentum of every AO. The ordering of $p$ functions is $(p_y,p_z,p_x)$ in the real-spherical convention of the library and is fixed by a test on a diatomic aligned with each axis.

\section{Population localization by Jacobi rotations}
\label{app:pm}
Populations are L\"owdin populations, $Q^A_{ij}=\sum_{\mu\in A}\tilde L_{\mu i}\tilde L_{\mu j}$ with $\tilde{\vb L}=\vb{S}^{1/2}\vb{L}$ (for the Pipek--Mezey comparison, Mulliken populations $Q^A_{ij}=\sum_{\mu\in A}\sum_\nu L_{\mu i}S_{\mu\nu}L_{\nu j}$, symmetrized). For a pair of orbitals $(i,j)$ in the current localized set, the exponent-2 case is handled in closed form: Rotating the pair by an angle $\gamma$,
\[
\vb{l}_i'=\cos\gamma\,\vb{l}_i+\sin\gamma\,\vb{l}_j,\qquad \vb{l}_j'=-\sin\gamma\,\vb{l}_i+\cos\gamma\,\vb{l}_j ,
\]
changes the functional \eqref{eq:pm} by $\Delta\mathcal{P}=A_{ij}+\sqrt{A_{ij}^2+B_{ij}^2}$ at the optimal angle, with
\begin{equation}
A_{ij}=\sum_A\Big[\big(Q^A_{ij}\big)^2-\tfrac14\big(Q^A_{ii}-Q^A_{jj}\big)^2\Big],\qquad
B_{ij}=\sum_A Q^A_{ij}\big(Q^A_{ii}-Q^A_{jj}\big),
\end{equation}
and the optimal angle given by $\cos4\gamma=-A_{ij}/\sqrt{A_{ij}^2+B_{ij}^2}$, $\sin4\gamma=B_{ij}/\sqrt{A_{ij}^2+B_{ij}^2}$ \cite{pm}. For the exponent-4 functional of the intrinsic bond orbitals the gain is a trigonometric polynomial of degree four in $4\gamma$ and the optimal angle is obtained from the closed form given by Knizia \cite{ibo} (or, equivalently, by a bracketed one-dimensional maximization per pair). A sweep applies this to every pair; disjoint pairs can be rotated simultaneously, which is how the implementation vectorizes a sweep without changing the sequence of rotations. Convergence is declared when $\Delta\mathcal{P}$ summed over a sweep falls below $10^{-8}$. The start is a pivoted Cholesky factorization of the block's density matrix in the L\"owdin basis, $\vb{P}=\vb{R}^{\!\top}\vb{R}$ with the pivot order fixed by the largest remaining diagonal, which yields a localized, non-iterative initial set \cite{cholesky}; with a fixed pair order the result is deterministic. With the exponent-2 Mulliken functional the aromatic $\pi$ space reached the sweep cap with the functional changing by $10^{-8}$--$10^{-6}$ per sweep; with the exponent-4 L\"owdin functional it converges.

\section{Derivation sketch of the Klopman--Salem equation}
\label{app:ks}
Consider one orbital $\psi_r$ (energy $E_r$) on fragment $A$ and one orbital $\psi_s$ (energy $E_s$) on fragment $B$, with interaction element $H_{rs}$ and overlap $S_{rs}$, both small. The $2\times2$ secular problem
\[
\begin{vmatrix} E_r-E & H_{rs}-ES_{rs}\\ H_{rs}-ES_{rs} & E_s-E\end{vmatrix}=0
\]
has, to second order in the small quantities, the roots
\begin{equation}
E_\pm\approx E_{r,s}\pm\frac{(H_{rs}-E_{r,s}S_{rs})^2}{E_r-E_s}\quad\text{(lower root near }E_r\text{ if }E_r<E_s).
\end{equation}
\emph{Filled--empty pair.} Two electrons occupy the lower root; the stabilization is $2(H_{rs}-E_rS_{rs})^2/(E_r-E_s)$. Expanding $\psi_r=\sum_a c_{ra}\chi_a$, $\psi_s=\sum_b c_{sb}\chi_b$ gives $H_{rs}-E_rS_{rs}=\sum_{ab}c_{ra}c_{sb}(H_{ab}-E_rS_{ab})$, and with the Wolfsberg--Helmholz form $H_{ab}=K S_{ab}(H_{aa}+H_{bb})/2$ the combination is proportional to $S_{ab}$ and is written $\beta_{ab}$. Summing over all filled orbitals of $A$ with all empty orbitals of $B$ and vice versa gives Term~(3).

\emph{Filled--filled pair.} Four electrons occupy both roots; the sum $2E_++2E_-$ differs from $2E_r+2E_s$ by a term proportional to $-(E_r+E_s)S_{rs}^2$ plus $2H_{rs}S_{rs}$-type corrections, which is positive (destabilizing) because $E_r,E_s<0$. Expanding in AOs and collecting the AO populations $q_a$ of the filled orbitals yields the form $-\sum_{ab}(q_a+q_b)\beta_{ab}S_{ab}$ of Term~(1).

\emph{Electrostatics.} The interaction of the unperturbed charge densities of $A$ and $B$, approximated by point charges at the atoms screened by a dielectric $\varepsilon$, gives Term~(2). Exchange and higher-order terms are dropped. The result is Eq.~\eqref{eq:ks-theory}; full derivations are in Klopman \cite{klopman}, Salem \cite{salem}, and in textbook form in Fleming \cite{fleming}.

\section{Bond--electron matrices: a worked example}
\label{app:be}
Methoxide attacking chloromethane, atoms ordered $(\mathrm{O},\mathrm{C},\mathrm{Cl})$ with hydrogens omitted for brevity. Reactant and product $\vb{B}$ matrices and their difference:
\[
\vb{B}_{\mathrm{reac}}=\begin{pmatrix}6&0&0\\0&0&1\\0&1&6\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\vb{B}_{\mathrm{prod}}=\begin{pmatrix}4&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&8\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\vb{R}=\begin{pmatrix}-2&+1&0\\+1&0&-1\\0&-1&+2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Here O carries three lone pairs (6 electrons) in the reactant and two in the product; Cl gains a lone pair. $\sum_{ij}R_{ij}=0$. The two arrows of the S$_\mathrm{N}$2 step are the two elementary matrices ``lone pair O $\to$ C'' ($R_{\mathrm{OO}}=-2$, $R_{\mathrm{OC}}=R_{\mathrm{CO}}=+1$) and ``bond C--Cl $\to$ Cl'' ($R_{\mathrm{CCl}}=R_{\mathrm{ClC}}=-1$, $R_{\mathrm{ClCl}}=+2$); their sum is $\vb{R}$. The model predicts $\vb{R}$ directly; this decomposition into two arrows is the reading shown to a chemist (Section~\ref{sec:interpret}). Formal charges follow: O goes from $6-6-1=-1$ (with its C--O bond included in the full matrix) to $0$; Cl from $0$ to $-1$. In the implementation the edit is performed on an RDKit molecule with explicit valence checks, which is equivalent to applying $\vb{R}$ and verifying that every row satisfies the octet constraints of its element.
