## Verdict

**The data layer is not safe to train on yet.** There are three blockers, plus five bugs that
change model inputs or labels and that all need a token, oriented-cache and sample rebuild anyway:

- the default split is per step, not per molecule, so the reported random-split numbers are
  optimistic;
- the cached artifacts carry no provenance; the current sample files are already stale against
  `steps.parquet`, and the next token rebuild will make the oriented cache misaligned without warning;
- three of the five textbook reactant pools are in the GBDT's training set.

The physics itself checks out: KS1 >= 0, the signs of KS2 and KS3, AO-level beta with shell-averaged
energies, a single 0.20 site rule, and multi-label marginalization in every metric. No diagnostic
label reaches a tensor. PM localization is deterministic. The problems are in splits, provenance,
invariance of a few inputs, and error handling.

Estimated effort to fix every blocker and bug: **about 38 engineering hours (about 5 working days),
plus about 25 CPU hours of rebuilds** (details at the end).

## Method and caveats

- Six parallel read-only reviewers, one per module, plus a cross-check: every blocker and the
  highest-ranked bugs below were re-run by hand. Evidence scripts are in the session scratchpad
  (`review/*_scripts/`). None of them is in the repo.
- **Compute.** The PMechDB GFN2 batch was running at 4 workers throughout. The host hard-reboots
  above that, so every measurement below ran one at a time as a single BLAS-pinned extra process
  (`OMP/OPENBLAS/MKL_NUM_THREADS=1`), which the user approved. Absolute timings are therefore from a
  loaded machine. Profiles used scratch output directories, never the real caches.
- **Spec numbering.** `docs/ksmech_architecture.tex` has 13 `\section`s, so "sections 12-16, 19-20"
  do not exist. The review uses §5 (Klopman-Salem decomposition), §8 (training objective),
  §9 (label alignment and step application), §10-11 (inference, complexity) and §12 (evaluation
  protocol).
- Line numbers are as of the working tree on 2026-10-21 19:00.

### Determinism of PM localization

- **Test.** 50 molecules with a current QM record were chosen as an even spread over the sorted
  SMILES. Tokens were built twice with `run_build(..., n_workers=1)` into two scratch directories,
  three seconds apart.
- **Result:** 50 / 50 files are byte-identical and 50 / 50 are array-identical. The zip timestamps
  do not differ.
- **Caveat** (finding O8): byte identity holds only at a fixed BLAS thread count. The pooled build
  pins one thread, but `--workers 1`, `orbitals show` and `textbook` do not. LMO signs are not
  canonicalized, and the token order among equal-energy LMOs is not tie-broken.

**Performance verdict on the requested patterns:**

| pattern | finding |
|---|---|
| Python loops over AO pairs | None in a hot path. The oriented kernel loops in Python over atom-pair **groups** (65k `_group_overlap` calls, each with an `interp1d` call). This is the main cost, but only on a cold cache. |
| per-pair recomputation of per-token quantities | Yes. `bonded_matrix` is rebuilt on every call, per acceptor per block per distance; so are `ao_energies` and `_Side.of` (P4, P5). `_weight_on` is called 1.5 M times (O4). 40 % of the fragment-pair blocks across steps are duplicates (77,303 blocks, 46,228 unique, A7). |
| repeated parquet reads | None. Samples and parquet are read once per command. |
| unvectorized Jacobi sweeps | No. Each round is vectorized over its n/2 disjoint pairs. But the dense `P @ X` (n_atoms × nao indicator) makes a sweep O(n² · nao · n_atoms). `np.add.reduceat` gives the same result, about 2.2× faster per call (O5). |
| O(N²) that should be O(N) in the sample builder | `valid_pairs` builds `own` with a double Python loop over occ × virt (0.33 s on a 140 × 124 pool). `site_excluded_matrix` does Python work per centre. Both are O(n_occ · n_virt) Python steps where a vectorized boolean product would do. The pair tensors themselves are O(N²) by design (spec §11). |

## Is the data layer safe to train on?

**No, not yet.** Three things must change first.

- **Splits (D1, D2, D3).** A model trained and evaluated on today's splits would report numbers
  inflated by molecule overlap: a third of the random-test steps reuse their main reactant from
  train. The split also changes under every re-ingest, and nothing records which split was used.
- **Provenance.** Nothing guarantees that a sample, its token files and its oriented blocks belong
  together. Every input fix in this review (Q1, O1, PK1) needs a token or oriented rebuild, and
  without provenance that rebuild produces silently misaligned features.
- **Inputs and labels (Q1, O1, PK1, A2, D5).** Four kinds of model input are not invariant to an
  arbitrary choice: the degenerate subspace (Q1, O1), the lab-frame orientation (PK1) and the atom
  numbering (D5). Separately, 3 % of labeled cells point at candidates the mask removes (A2). These
  are small per-item effects, but they are systematic.

Once these are fixed and the caches rebuilt, the core is sound:

- the physics signs and forms match the spec;
- the alignment takes its sites from the dataset arrows;
- multi-label sets survive into the tensors;
- no diagnostic label leaks into the model;
- localization is reproducible;
- train-only standardization is in place.

It is then safe to train on after one more check: the overlap assertion above rerun on the new
splits.
